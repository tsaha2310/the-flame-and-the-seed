A battery discharging is a redox running downhill, electrons falling from the giver to the taker through your fridge. Recharging is the same reaction pushed uphill. The mains supplies a voltage bigger than the cell's own and forces electrons back the other way, turning the products back into reactants. It is electrolysis, the same tool that split water in Book I. To recharge is to run a battery's reaction backwards by paying more free energy in than you will get out.

Energy in is always more than energy out. Some is lost as heat in the plates and wires each way. A lead-acid battery gives back about four-fifths. Nothing gives back all.

And a recharged battery is a fall stored for later. The inverter's battery is a marble carried up the stairs by the mains at noon, waiting to roll down during the evening cut.

The gate panel. Iron in damp air makes a tiny cell of its own: one patch of the metal gives electrons, the water carries ions, another patch hands the electrons to oxygen. The gate is discharging itself, slowly, to no purpose, and the product is rust. That self-discharge of a metal in its surroundings is corrosion. Salt speeds it because salt water is a better bridge.

**Recharging pushes a redox uphill by paying more than you get back. Corrosion is a battery nobody built, discharging into rust.**
