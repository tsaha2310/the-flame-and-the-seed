Some of the eggs, a few in a hundred, grew into complete tadpoles, and some of those into frogs. A gut cell's nucleus had made muscle, nerve, skin, blood, eye. Every gene was still there. Nothing had been thrown away.

So a gut cell and a nerve cell have the same tape. What differs is which genes are copied. In the gut cell, the genes for gut proteins are loose and copied; the genes for nerve proteins are wound tight and silent, from VI.5 L01. Put the nucleus into an egg, and the egg's own proteins unwind the chromatin and reset the switches, and the whole tape is available again.

Same book. Different pages open. The Lens shows it: the gut nucleus's chromatin, tight in patches, loosens across the board inside the egg.

Gurdon shared a Nobel Prize fifty years later, in 2012. The cloning of a sheep in 1996 was his experiment done with a mammal.

**Every cell holds the whole tape. Cells differ because different genes are switched on. A gut cell's nucleus, given a fresh start, can make a whole frog.**
