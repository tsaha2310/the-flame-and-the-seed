In the wire, what moves is electrons. Copper atoms sit still in rows, and a few of their outer electrons are free to drift. The battery pushes them along. That drift is the current.

In the salt water, there are no free electrons. What moves is atoms that carry a charge. When salt dissolves, it breaks into sodium atoms that have lost one electron, so they are positive, and chlorine atoms that have gained one, so they are negative. A charged atom is called an ion. Positive ions drift one way, negative ions the other, and both together carry the current.

Sugar dissolves into whole molecules with no charge. They float about and carry nothing.

The battery's push has a name: voltage. For now, think of it as how hard the battery shoves. More voltage, more current, brighter LED. There is a better picture of voltage coming in III.9, on the Hill.

**A current is moving charge. In a wire the charge is electrons. In salt water it is ions. Sugar water has neither.**
