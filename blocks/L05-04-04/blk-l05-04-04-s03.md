Enzyme 1 cuts starch at alpha links, works best near pH 7, and is found in saliva and in the gut. It is amylase. It is the cutter from the chewed roti. Its pocket fits the alpha link, and that pocket is why rice feeds you and paper does not.

Enzyme 2 cuts proteins, works best near pH 2, and does nothing at pH 7. It is pepsin, from the stomach. It is made by the stomach's own cells, which are protein. So it is made asleep: a longer chain with an extra tail folded over its pocket. In the stomach's acid, from III.7, the tail is cut off and the pocket opens. Pepsin is born inactive and woken by acid, in the one place where acid is.

Enzyme 3 takes glucose and ATP together. It moves the ATP's end phosphate onto the glucose, tagging it. It is hexokinase: "hexose", a six-carbon sugar, and "kinase", a phosphate-mover. Every cell has it. It is the first step of burning glucose, and it is the worked case of coupling for tomorrow's module. Tagged glucose is charged, so it cannot leave through the wall, from V.2; the cell has clamped it.

**Amylase cuts starch in the mouth. Pepsin cuts protein in the stomach and is born asleep. Hexokinase tags glucose with a phosphate from ATP, and every cell starts burning sugar this way.**
