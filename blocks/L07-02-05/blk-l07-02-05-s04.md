Three lessons.

The sodium gate is the wave. Plug it and there is no message at all. The pufferfish's toxin does this, as do some snake and cone-snail venoms, and a local anaesthetic at the dentist does it on purpose, to one small region, for an hour.

The refractory period is direction. Without it, a nerve rings instead of carrying.

Myelin is speed. Without it, a body-length message takes a second, which for a big animal is too slow to live.

And the race. Electrons in copper move a signal at near light speed, from I.4. A nerve's signal is a wave of proteins changing shape, each in a millisecond, jumping gap to gap, and at every synapse a molecule must diffuse across. Fifty metres a second is the best a body manages. It is a million times slower than a wire and it is enough, because the body is not a metre wide and the relays can compute at every gap.

**A nerve is chemistry in a chain: gates, lockouts, sleeves and synapses, each a shape that can be blocked. That is why it is a million times slower than a wire, and why a milligram of the right molecule can stop it.**
