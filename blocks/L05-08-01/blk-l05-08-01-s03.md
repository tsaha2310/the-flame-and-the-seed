Inside the microbe, glycolysis runs as in V.6: glucose to two pyruvate, two coins net, two buckets filled. With no oxygen, there is no staircase to empty the buckets. So the microbe does something blunt. It hands the bucket's electrons to the pyruvate itself. NADH gives its pair to pyruvate, becomes NAD+, and is empty again. The pyruvate that took the electrons becomes an acid, or an alcohol and a gas, depending on the microbe.

Running glycolysis with the buckets emptied onto pyruvate instead of oxygen is fermentation. Its product, lactic acid, is why the batter is sour. The gas, carbon dioxide from the yeasts, is why it doubled and why the idlis have holes.

Fermentation makes no extra coins. The two coins from glycolysis are all there is. Its job is not energy. Fermentation hands NAD+ back, so that glycolysis can run again and make those two coins again. That is the whole of it.

**Fermentation is glycolysis kept running without oxygen, by dumping the buckets' electrons onto pyruvate. Fermentation hands NAD+ back. It makes no coins of its own.**

Warm batter ferments faster, from III.6's collisions and V.4's optimum. Salt slows it by osmosis, from III.5, pulling water from the microbes.
