A solution that holds all the solute it can at that temperature is saturated. Add more and it sits. Sugar saturates cold water at about 200 g per 100 g of water, a syrup. Salt saturates at about 36 g, and it barely changes with temperature.

For most solids, warmer water holds more. More jiggle means water molecules can pull particles off the crystal faster than they fall back. Cool it and the extra comes back out as crystals, which is how Ajji's saucer grew its cubes in Book II.

For gases it is the other way round. A gas molecule in water is held only loosely, and warmer water's jiggle shakes it out. Cold water holds more carbon dioxide, so the cold drink keeps its fizz. Cold water holds more oxygen too, which is why fish in a warm, still pond gasp at the surface.

How much solute is in how much solution is its concentration. Chemists usually count it in moles per litre, which is called molarity. A litre of sea water holds about half a mole of salt: half-molar.

**Warm water dissolves more solid and less gas. A solution that can take no more is saturated, and its concentration is measured in moles per litre.**
