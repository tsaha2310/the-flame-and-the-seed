A zigzag with four bends and two ends is six carbons. Each end carbon has one carbon neighbour, so three hydrogens. Each middle carbon has two carbon neighbours, so two hydrogens. Six carbons, fourteen hydrogens.

A branch is a corner with three lines meeting. That carbon has three carbon neighbours and one hydrogen.

A ring of six corners is six carbons, each with two carbon neighbours and two hydrogens. Twelve hydrogens.

A double line is a double bond. The two carbons on it spend two bonds on each other, so each has one fewer hydrogen than it would in a chain.

**In a skeleton drawing, every corner and end is a carbon. The hydrogens are whatever fills each carbon up to four bonds.**

From here on, molecules are drawn this way. When a drawing shows a letter, it is an atom that is not carbon, and that is exactly where the next rungs of the ladder go.
