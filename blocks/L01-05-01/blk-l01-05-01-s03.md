Laid out by mass with new rows where the pattern repeats, the cards form a table. Each column holds elements that behave alike. Each row runs from a soft, fizzy metal on the left to a sharp gas on the right.

This is the periodic table. Periodic means "repeating at intervals". A column is a group: a family of look-alikes. A row is a period.

Mendeleev did something braver than sorting. Where the pattern needed an element that nobody had found, he left a gap and predicted its mass and properties. Below aluminium, he predicted "eka-aluminium": mass about 68, a soft metal that melts easily.

Six years later a French chemist found gallium. Mass 70. A metal so soft it melts in your hand.

**Sorted by mass, the elements repeat in families. The repeats are real enough to predict elements nobody has seen.**
