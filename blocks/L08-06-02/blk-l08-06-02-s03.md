The widest drawer in the old scheme is a kingdom. Five were taught for decades: animals, plants, fungi, single-celled eukaryotes such as amoebas, and the prokaryotes, all bacteria together. The scheme is a human filing system, made from what could be seen under a microscope, and it was always under revision. The amoeba drawer was a catch-all for anything single-celled with a nucleus, and it was never one family.

Then the tape. In 1977 Carl Woese compared the RNA of ribosomes, which every cell has and which changes slowly, across everything he could find. Some microbes from hot springs and salt lakes, prokaryotes by their look, were as far from ordinary bacteria in their RNA as bacteria are from us. The top drawer was redrawn as three domains: Bacteria; Archaea, the hot-spring line; and Eukarya, everything with a nucleus, from yeast to tiger. The three-domain scheme sits above the kingdoms, and the kingdoms of animals, plants and fungi are three branches inside Eukarya.

Both schemes are in use. Five kingdoms is the older, looks-based filing; three domains is what the tape says. A filing system is a tool, and tools get revised when the evidence does.

**Seven levels, kingdom to species, file every organism. The five-kingdom scheme is a looks-based filing system under revision; the tape redrew the top into three domains, and the old prokaryote box turned out to hold two lines as far apart as either is from us.**
