After one division: a single band, halfway. The whole-kept idea is dead at once; it predicted a heavy band and a light one. Both remaining ideas predict halfway, so the second division decides.

After two divisions: two bands, one halfway and one light, equal. Half-and-half predicts exactly that: each half-heavy helix unzips, each old strand gets a new light partner, so half the helices are half-heavy and half are all light. The patchwork idea predicts a single band drifting toward light, with no separate light band. It is dead too.

The copying of DNA is replication. Each strand of the helix is a template, from IV.8 L04; the helix unzips; a new partner strand is built on each by pairing, A to T and G to C; and the result is two helices, each with one old strand and one new. Half-conserved, half new: semi-conservative. The pairing in Book IV was the copying mechanism, exactly as Watson and Crick noticed.

**DNA replication is semi-conservative: the helix unzips, each old strand templates a new partner, and each daughter helix keeps one old strand and one new. Meselson and Stahl proved it by weighing DNA with heavy nitrogen.**
