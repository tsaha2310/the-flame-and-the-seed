Carbon dioxide is straight. Its two polar bonds pull in exactly opposite directions. Add the arrows and they cancel to nothing. The molecule as a whole has no negative end and no positive end.

Water is bent. Its two polar bonds both lean toward the oxygen, which sits at the corner of the bend. Add the arrows and they do not cancel. They add up to one net arrow pointing at the oxygen. The oxygen corner is slightly negative and the two hydrogens are slightly positive.

A molecule with a net arrow, a negative side and a positive side, is a polar molecule. Water is one. Ammonia is one. Carbon dioxide is not, even though its bonds are. Methane is not, and its bonds are barely polar to start with.

**Polar bonds make a polar molecule only if the shape stops them cancelling. Water is polar because it is bent.**

Now the ghee. Oil is long chains of carbon with hydrogens, bonds that are barely polar, arranged so that what little lean there is cancels. Oil molecules have no charged sides at all.
