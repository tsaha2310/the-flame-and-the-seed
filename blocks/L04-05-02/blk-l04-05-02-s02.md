> **The Lens says:** Beads with their side chains, the core drawn as a repeating unit. The water that leaves at each join is real. The chain is drawn straight for now; it will not stay that way.

Take an oily bead. Bring its carboxyl to the amine of a polar bead. Join. Catch the water.

Add a plus bead. Then a minus. Then any five you like.

Read the sequence the bench prints, one letter per bead. Rotate the chain. Notice which end still has a free amine and which a free carboxyl.

Build a second chain of the same ten beads in a different order. Compare the two sequence strings.
