A large region with a characteristic climate and the community of living things fitted to it is a biome. The same loops as the bottle run in each, with the dials set differently.

The Thar. Almost no water, so few producers, small and spiny, with deep roots and thick skins that keep turgor, from V.2 L04, through months of drought. Short webs; animals that hide by day. Decomposers barely work because nothing is wet, so what dies dries and lasts.

The Western Ghats. Rain and warmth together, so producers in layers a hundred feet tall, leaves kept all year, and webs of enormous length and variety. Decomposers work fast: a leaf is gone in weeks. Nutrients are in the living things, not the thin red soil, which is why cleared forest turns poor in a few seasons.

The Himalaya. Cold, so slow everything: small producers, short summers, animals with fat and fur, from IV.3, and decomposers that work only in the brief warmth. Dead matter piles up as peat and thin soil.

The Sundarbans. Salt water twice a day, so producers that pump salt out, from V.2 L03, on roots that breathe above the mud. Webs that run from mangrove leaf litter through crabs and fish to a tiger that swims.

Selection, from VIII.5, has fitted every living thing to its dials. Move a Thar plant to the Ghats and it rots; move a Ghats plant to the Thar and it dries.

**A biome is a region of climate and the community fitted to it. The same loops of carbon, nitrogen and water run everywhere; rain and temperature set how fast, and selection has fitted each place's living things to its dials.**
