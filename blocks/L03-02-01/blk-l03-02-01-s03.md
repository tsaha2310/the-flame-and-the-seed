You cannot count rice by hand for a wedding. You weigh ten grains, weigh the sack, divide. It works because grains of a kind weigh about the same.

Atoms of a kind weigh exactly the same, near enough. So chemists do what the shopkeeper does. They set hydrogen, the lightest atom, at 1. Carbon weighs 12 times as much, so carbon is 12. Oxygen is 16. Sodium is 23. Chlorine is 35.5, because chlorine comes as a mix of two isotopes and the average is not a whole number. Iron is 56.

That number is the relative atomic mass. It is a comparison, with no unit: "how many hydrogens would balance one of these".

Now the fixed recipe from Book I makes sense. Water is two hydrogens and one oxygen. Two times 1 is 2. One times 16 is 16. Two to sixteen is one to eight. That is why water is always 1 gram of hydrogen for every 8 of oxygen.

**Every atom of a kind weighs the same, so you can count atoms the way a shopkeeper counts coins: weigh, and divide.**
