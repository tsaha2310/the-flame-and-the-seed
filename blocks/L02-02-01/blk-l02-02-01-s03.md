Two balloons at a knot point opposite ways: 180 degrees. Three spread into a flat Y: 120 degrees. Four point at the corners of a pyramid with a triangle base: about 109 degrees between any two.

The balloons push apart because they take up space and cannot overlap. Electron pairs push apart for a stronger reason. Every electron is negative, and like charges repel. Two pairs around the same atom get as far from each other as they can.

A shared pair, or any pair, is not a dot or a ring. It is a cloud: a blurry region where the two electrons are most likely to be. The proper name for such a region is an orbital. The simplest orbital is a round cloud, called s. The next kind is a pair of lobes on opposite sides of the nucleus, called p.

Honesty card, said again: electrons do not circle. A shell is an energy level. The cloud shows where the electron is likely to be. The rings you drew in Book I were a way to count, not a picture.

**Electron pairs around an atom push each other as far apart as they can go. That pushing sets the shape of every molecule.**
