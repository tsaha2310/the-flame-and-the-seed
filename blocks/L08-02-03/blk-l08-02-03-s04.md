A double hybrid, TtRr, gives one factor for height and one for shape to each child, each chosen at random, and the two choices are independent. So it can give TR, Tr, tR or tr, each a quarter of the time. Two parents, four choices each, sixteen boxes.

Colour the boxes. Tall-round, anything with a T and an R: nine. Tall-wrinkled, T and rr: three. Short-round, tt and R: three. Short-wrinkled, tt and rr: one. Nine, three, three, one. That is what Mendel counted, and what the thousand-plant run shows, with scatter.

The height factor and the shape factor did not travel together. The tall-round parent gave tall with wrinkled as often as tall with round. Each pair of factors sorts on its own, as if the other did not exist. That was Mendel's second finding, and it means the factors are separate things, not one bundle.

**Two traits sort independently. Each gives three to one, and the two multiply: nine to three to three to one. The factors for different traits travel separately.**

III.8's counting again: independent chances multiply. Two coins, four outcomes; two traits, sixteen boxes.
