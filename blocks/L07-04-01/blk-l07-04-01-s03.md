Each ant that finds food lays a molecule on the ground as it walks home. Other ants, meeting the smell, follow it toward where it is strongest, lay their own drops, and strengthen it. A molecule released by one animal that changes the behaviour of another of its kind is a pheromone. It is VII.3's signal molecule, carried on a floor instead of in water.

The short route wins by itself. Ants on the short route make the round trip sooner, so more drops land on it per minute than on the long route. Evaporation, from II.4, takes drops off both routes at the same rate. The short one gains faster than it loses; the long one loses faster than it gains. In a few minutes the whole colony is on the short route, and no ant ever compared the two.

Evaporation is the eraser. Too slow, and a trail to food that has gone keeps the colony walking to nothing. Too fast, and no trail survives long enough to be reinforced. The rate is tuned to the ant's world.

**A pheromone is a molecule one animal leaves for another of its kind. Ants lay it and follow it, and because short routes get reinforced faster than they evaporate, the shortest path draws itself.**

Moths find mates by a pheromone across kilometres of air, with receptors so sensitive a few molecules suffice. Bees release an alarm pheromone when they sting, which calls the hive to sting the same spot. All are molecule meets receptor, from VII.1, between bodies instead of between cells.
