Six cups of besan is three batches. Two cups of ghee is two batches. Five cups of sugar is five batches. The kitchen can make two batches, 24 laddoos, and then the ghee is gone. Two cups of besan and three of sugar sit unused.

The ingredient that runs out first sets the limit. It is called the limiting reagent. Everything else is in excess.

In the molecule kitchen: 3 moles of hydrogen would make 3 moles of water, if the oxygen holds out. 2 moles of oxygen could make 4 moles of water, if the hydrogen holds out. Hydrogen runs out first. You get 3 moles of water, 54 g, and half a mole of oxygen is left over.

**A reaction stops when the first reactant runs out. That reactant is the limiting reagent, and it sets how much product you get.**

This is the fixed recipe from Book I, seen from the cook's side. Extra hydrogen does not make extra water. It just sits there.
