The chain folds because of the pulls you already know.

Water molecules pull on each other so hard that oily side chains are squeezed together into the middle, out of the water. That is the biggest push, and it happens first. Polar side chains are happy in water and stay outside.

A plus bead and a minus bead pull toward each other, low on the charge Hill. Where the fold lets them meet, they hold.

Hydrogen bonds form between the chain's own backbone, from every peptide bond's N-H to a nearby C=O. Where the chain twists so that each bond lines up four beads along, it makes a spiral: a helix. Where it lies in strips side by side, it makes a sheet. Most proteins are helices and sheets packed together with oily beads inside.

And the two sulphur beads: bring them close and their sulphurs bond to each other, a real covalent link across the fold. That is a sulphur bridge. It locks the fold like a staple.

The finished shape is the fold. The sequence chooses it: oily beads here and polar beads there, so the chain collapses one way and not another. Same sequence, same fold, every time.

**A protein folds because water squeezes its oily beads inside, charges pair, and the backbone hydrogen-bonds into helices and sheets. The sequence decides the fold.**
