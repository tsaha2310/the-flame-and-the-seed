Kabir's rule puts hydrogen sulphide above water. Reality puts it 160 degrees below. He crosses it out himself.

**Predict first:** which pair breaks Kabir's rule?

<!-- STEP id="run" -->
**Turn the dial.** Methane weighs 16, water weighs 18. Nearly equal, but water boils 262 degrees higher. Then hydrogen sulphide, heavier than water, boils at minus 60. The rule fails twice.

<!-- STEP id="nails" -->
**What this nails:** Mass matters a little, through dispersion forces, but the kind of pull between molecules matters far more.
