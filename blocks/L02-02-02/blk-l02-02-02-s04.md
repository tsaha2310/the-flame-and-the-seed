Carbon dioxide settles into a straight line. Two groups of clouds around the carbon, and two things push furthest apart by pointing opposite ways. The shape is called linear.

Boron trifluoride settles into a flat triangle, 120 degrees. Three groups. The shape is called trigonal, from "three corners".

Methane settles into the four-balloon shape. Four groups, about 109 degrees. The shape is called tetrahedral, from "four faces", because if you join the four hydrogens with lines you get a pyramid with four triangular faces.

The rule you found with balloons now has a table:

| groups of clouds | shape | angle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | linear | 180 |
| 3 | trigonal | 120 |
| 4 | tetrahedral | about 109 |

A double bond counts as one group. It is two pairs travelling together.

**Count the groups of clouds around the central atom. Two is a line, three is a triangle, four is a tetrahedron.**
