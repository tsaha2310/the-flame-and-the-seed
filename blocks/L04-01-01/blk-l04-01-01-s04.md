The crystals matched. Same shape, same melting point, and, once molecules could be seen, the same molecule: one carbon, one oxygen, two nitrogens, four hydrogens. Urea from a mineral is urea from a kidney. There is no way to tell them apart, because there is nothing to tell.

Wohler wrote to his teacher: "I can make urea without needing a kidney." It was the first time a molecule that only life had made was made without life. Within decades, chemists had made sugars, fats and dyes from coal and air.

The vital-force hypothesis said this was impossible. It was tested. It failed.

The old name for the compounds of living things stuck anyway: organic compounds. Today it just means compounds built around carbon, whoever or whatever made them. The oil in the diya is organic. The plastic of Kabir's torch is organic. Every molecule in the seed is organic.

**The molecules of life are ordinary chemistry. A flask can make them, so no special force is needed to make them.**

Notice what has fallen and what has not. Life's molecules need no vital force. Whether life itself does is a different question, and it stays open for now.
