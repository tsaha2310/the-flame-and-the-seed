Dahi and sprinting muscle: sour, no gas, no alcohol. In the Lens, pyruvate takes the two electrons straight onto its middle carbon, and the carbon-oxygen double bond there becomes a hydroxyl, from IV.3. Three carbons in, three out. The product is lactic acid, which in the body's water is its ion, lactate. The dahi's microbes make it from milk sugar; your muscle makes it from glucose when the staircase cannot keep up.

Batter and dough: gas, and a faint alcohol smell. In the Lens, the yeast's pyruvate first loses a carbon as carbon dioxide, and the two-carbon piece that is left takes the electrons. Three carbons in, two out plus a gas. The product is ethanol, the alcohol in spirit lamps and in wine, made from grape sugar by the same yeast. The carbon dioxide is the bubbles in bread and in the batter.

Two ways to dump electrons onto pyruvate. Both hand NAD+ back. Neither makes a coin. Which one a cell uses depends on which enzymes it has.

**Lactate: pyruvate takes the electrons whole, three carbons, sour. Ethanol: pyruvate loses a carbon as gas first, then takes the electrons, two carbons, alcohol. Both empty the buckets.**

Your muscle sends its lactate to the liver, which, with oxygen and coins, turns it back into glucose. Nothing is wasted that can be recovered.
