You are about two-thirds water. Every bit of heat your muscles make spreads into that water, and water's high heat capacity means a great deal of heat produces only a small rise. Sweat carries away the rest by evaporation, which you saw cool the matka.

If your water had oil's heat capacity, the same heat would raise your temperature twice as fast. The fever line, around 39 degrees, would arrive after a short run.

This is the endothermic side of the ledger, working for you. Loosening water's hydrogen bonds soaks up heat. Evaporating sweat soaks up much more. Both are grips taking in energy so your jiggle does not have to.

**A body is mostly water because water soaks up heat without warming much. That is what lets a living thing run hot chemistry without cooking itself.**

The same trick, at planet scale: the oceans soak up summer and give it back in winter. Coastal cities are gentle. Deserts swing forty degrees between noon and midnight.
