Three turns. Three carbon dioxides in. Three acceptors, fifteen carbons, become six three-carbon pieces, eighteen carbons. Fifteen of those carbons go back to rebuild three acceptors. Three carbons, one three-carbon piece, are the profit. Three in, three out. The accountant is satisfied.

The three-carbon piece that leaves is a small sugar with a phosphate tag, called G3P. It is half a glucose. Two of them, joined, make one glucose, from IV.4. And G3P is the start of everything: glucose, sucrose for the sap, starch for the seed's store, cellulose for the walls, and, with a few more steps, fatty acids and amino acids.

The cost. Each turn spends ATP and NADPH at two points: coins to tag pieces and reshape the acceptor, and NADPH to push electrons onto the carbon, from III.3, raising it from plus four toward zero. Per G3P exported: nine ATP and six NADPH. That is what the light reactions were filling.

**Three turns of the Calvin cycle take in three carbon dioxides and export one G3P, half a glucose, spending nine ATP and six NADPH. The rest rebuilds the acceptor.**
