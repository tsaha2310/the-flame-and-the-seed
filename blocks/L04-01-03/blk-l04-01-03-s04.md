Silicon is bigger than carbon. Its outer electrons sit a shell further out, so its bonds to itself are longer and weaker. A silicon chain is a chain with a weak link at every link.

Worse, silicon's bond to oxygen is very strong, far stronger than its bond to itself. In any world with oxygen, silicon chains fall apart into silicon-oxygen bonds. The Earth's crust is exactly this: silicon and oxygen locked together as sand and rock.

And the oxide. Carbon dioxide is a small molecule, held to its neighbours only by weak pulls, from II.3. A gas. It leaves. Silicon dioxide is a giant web of shared pairs, like diamond, from II.4. A solid. It stays.

A living thing burns its fuel and must get rid of the ash. Carbon's ash is a gas you breathe out. Silicon's ash is a rock.

**Carbon builds long, strong chains and its waste is a gas. Silicon's chains are weak and its waste is sand. That is why life is carbon.**
