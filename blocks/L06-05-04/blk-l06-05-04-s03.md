Kabir watches a one-letter splice wreck a protein, then watches a bacterium make one in a minute.

"Faster without a nucleus."

"Faster, and no chance to edit. Two ways to be a cell."

**Predict first:** a splice one letter early: what does the ribosome make? And in the bacterium: how much sooner is the protein made?

<!-- STEP id="run" -->
**Turn the dial.** One letter early or late, and the join shifts the frame, from VI.4 L03. Every bead after the join is wrong and a stop appears within a few codons. A protein that lost half its chain. The splicing machine's exactness is not optional. Then the bacterium: ribosomes land on the copy's front end while the copyist is still writing its back end, and the first protein is finished about a minute after copying starts. The plant cell, with splicing, capping, tailing and export, takes several minutes longer. The bacterium is faster; the plant cell has a room in which to edit.

<!-- STEP id="nails" -->
**What this nails:** A join off by one is a frameshift, so splicing must land on the letter. And a nucleus costs time: bacteria couple writing and reading in one room and are quick; eukaryotes separate them and can edit, splice and choose.
