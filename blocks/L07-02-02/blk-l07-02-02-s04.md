The gates are voltage-gated channels: channel proteins from V.2 whose fold shifts when the voltage across the wall changes, opening or closing the tube. A sodium one is shut at minus seventy and snaps open at about minus fifty-five.

A small poke raises the voltage a little; it does not reach minus fifty-five; the gates stay shut; the nudge fades. A poke that reaches the threshold opens the sodium gates at that spot. Sodium floods in down its gradient, carrying positive charge, and the voltage at that spot flips to plus forty in a millisecond. That flip raises the voltage next door past threshold; those gates open; sodium floods there. The wave runs along the cable, gate by gate, without weakening, because each spot fires with its own stored sodium gradient, not with the poke's energy.

At each spot, the sodium gates shut on their own after a millisecond, and slower potassium gates open. Potassium rushes out, and the voltage falls back to rest. The spot is quiet again, a little behind the wave.

That travelling flip of voltage is an action potential. It is all or none: below threshold, nothing; above, a full wave, always the same size. A stronger stimulus does not make a bigger wave. It makes more waves per second.

**An action potential is a wave of voltage-gated sodium channels opening one after another along the cable. Below threshold nothing travels; above it a full-sized wave runs the whole length. All or none.**
