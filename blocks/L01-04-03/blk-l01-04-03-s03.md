One tube fills twice as fast as the other. When you have two volumes in one tube, you have one in the other. Always 2 to 1.

The bigger tube pops with a squeak when a flame touches it. That gas is hydrogen, the lightest element, one proton per atom. The smaller tube makes a glowing splint burst back into flame. That is oxygen, which you met in the sealed jar.

Splitting a compound with a current is called electrolysis. It is the hardest tool from the shelf lesson, and now you have seen what it does.

Two volumes of hydrogen, one of oxygen. And back in I.1, water was 1 gram of hydrogen for every 8 of oxygen. Both are true at once. Hydrogen atoms are sixteen times lighter than oxygen atoms, so two of them together weigh an eighth of one oxygen.

**Water comes apart into two parts hydrogen and one part oxygen, by count. The mass recipe and the count recipe are the same fact.**
