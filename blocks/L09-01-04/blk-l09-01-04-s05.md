Ajji closes the notebook and pushes it across the table to you.

"It is yours," she says. "The lens too. The last page is back in, and you have written the one after it."

Kabir has been quiet for the whole duel, which is the longest he has ever been quiet.

"I did not win," he says.

"You did not lose either. Every line you broke was a line that needed breaking. That is what the second chair is for."

He takes the seed out of his pocket, the one he saved, and looks at it.

"Next monsoon," he says, "I get the lens. And I am going to ask about the virus."

The diya burns down. Nobody blows it out. It goes, the way a flame goes, when the oil is gone: all at once, with nothing kept, and a thread of smoke that drifts out over the garden, toward the leaves.
