Where one neuron's cable ends and the next begins, there is a gap a fiftieth of a micrometre wide. The wave cannot cross it: an ion flip on one side does nothing to gates on the other. The junction, with its gap, is a synapse.

The wave arrives at the tip. Voltage-gated calcium channels there open, calcium rushes in, and calcium makes the vesicles waiting at the tip fuse with the wall: exocytosis, from V.3 L03. They spill a small molecule into the gap. The molecule diffuses across in a fraction of a millisecond and fits receptors on the next neuron, from VII.1. Those receptors are channels; the fit opens them; ions flow; the next neuron's voltage rises past threshold; a new wave starts. A molecule spat across a gap to start the next wave is a neurotransmitter.

The circuit: fingertip to spinal cord, one synapse, spinal cord to muscle, one synapse. The muscle's receptors open channels, the muscle cell fires its own wave, and from V.5, myosin pulls. Touch to pull, about twenty milliseconds. That short loop is a reflex arc. A branch from the spinal cord carries the news up to the brain, which takes longer, so the hand is moving before the hurt arrives.

**A synapse is a gap between neurons. The arriving wave makes the tip spit a neurotransmitter by exocytosis; it fits receptors across the gap and starts the next wave. Fingertip to spine to muscle is a reflex arc.**

Every medicine for pain, sleep, mood and seizures acts at a synapse. And so did the botulism toxin from V.3 L04.
