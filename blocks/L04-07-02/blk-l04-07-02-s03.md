A and U fit. Two hydrogen bonds form between their edges: a giver on one meets a taker on the other, twice. G and C fit better: three hydrogen bonds.

The other four pairs do not fit. Their givers face givers, or their takers face takers, or the groups are the wrong distance apart. Nothing forms, or one weak bond that does not hold.

This is base pairing: A with U, G with C. It comes from the exact positions of the edge groups on each base, and from II.3's hydrogen bond, which needs a hydrogen on one side and an oxygen or nitrogen on the other, close and lined up.

And there is a second fact. A and G are big, two rings. C and U are small, one ring. Each working pair is one big and one small, and the two working pairs are the same width. A big with a big would be too wide. A small with a small would be too narrow. Tomorrow's module hangs on that width.

**A pairs with U by two hydrogen bonds. G pairs with C by three. Every pair is one big base and one small, so every pair is the same width.**
