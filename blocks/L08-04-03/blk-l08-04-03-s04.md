Snapdragons: the hybrid has one pigment-enzyme copy, half the enzyme, half the pigment, from yesterday's dosage dial. Pink. Cross pinks: one red, two pink, one white. Neither allele hides. When a hybrid is intermediate between the two pure types, that is incomplete dominance. It is not blending: the reds and whites come back whole in the grandchildren.

Blood group: one gene, three common alleles. Allele A adds one kind of sugar to a protein on the red cell's surface, from IV.4 and V.3; allele B adds a different sugar; allele O adds none. AA and AO cells carry the A sugar: group A. BB and BO: group B. OO: group O. AB carries both sugars, and both show, fully: group AB. When both alleles show fully and separately in a hybrid, that is codominance. The system is called ABO. It matters because the body's defences attack red cells carrying a sugar the body does not have, which is why a transfusion must match.

Height: with one gene, three heights. With three, seven. With a hundred genes each adding a little, plus food and health in childhood, the chart is a smooth hill. Height looks blended and is a hundred particles, from VIII.2 L04. Most traits you can see are like this: many genes plus environment.
