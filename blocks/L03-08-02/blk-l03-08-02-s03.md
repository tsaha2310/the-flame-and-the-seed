Two things push a change. Heat given out pushes it forward, because the energy released spreads into the surroundings, and that spreading has many ways too. Ways gained inside the system push it forward directly. And temperature sets how much the inside ways count: at high temperature, a gain in ways is worth a lot; at low temperature, little.

Add the two pushes, weighted by temperature, and you get a single number: how far downhill the change is, all things counted. That number is the free energy. If free energy falls, the change runs by itself. If it rises, the change does not.

Ice melting takes in heat, which pushes back. It gains ways, which pushes forward. At minus 10, the heat cost wins and ice stays ice. At plus 10, the ways gain, now worth more, wins and ice melts. At exactly zero they balance, and ice and water sit together, at equilibrium.

A candle: heat given out, ways gained, both forward. Downhill at any temperature. That is why burning is so common and so final.

**From now on, the Hill also counts spreading out. The true height is free energy: heat given out plus ways gained, with ways weighed by temperature.**

This is the upgrade. Every Hill you have drawn since Book I was the heat-only version. It was right whenever ways did not change much, which for most kitchen reactions is close enough. From here, it is the full one.
