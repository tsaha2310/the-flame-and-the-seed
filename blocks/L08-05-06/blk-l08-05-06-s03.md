DNA is a tape that cannot do anything. Protein is a machine that cannot be copied. Neither can start alone. RNA can do a little of both: it carries a message, from IV.7, and it folds into tools, from IV.7 L03 and VI.4 L02, where the ribosome's core is RNA to this day.

So the hypothesis: before cells, before DNA, before protein, there were RNA strands in warm water that folded into shapes that could copy RNA strands, including themselves, by pairing. Copying with errors gave variation. Copiers that copied faster or more accurately became commoner. Selection ran on molecules, with no wall around them. That is the RNA world.

The evidence for it: the ribosome's RNA heart; the RNA tools still at the centre of every cell; ATP being an RNA nucleotide, from IV.6; and RNA strands built in laboratories that can copy other strands, if not yet themselves completely. The evidence against it: nobody has made a full self-copier, and the chemistry of making the first nucleotides without help is hard. It is a hypothesis, and one of several.

**The RNA world is a hypothesis: before cells, RNA strands that folded into copiers copied themselves with errors, and selection ran on molecules. Supported by the RNA at the heart of every cell; not yet proven; not the only idea.**
