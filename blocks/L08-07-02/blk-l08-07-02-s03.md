Three patterns.

Both gain: mutualism, from yesterday.

One gains and the other is unaffected: commensalism, "eating at the same table". The remora rides the shark and eats what it drops; the shark's growth is the same with or without it. Most of the bacteria on your skin eat what your skin sheds and neither help nor harm. An orchid growing on a tree's branch takes light and rain and nothing from the tree.

One gains and the other pays: parasitism. The koel's chick is raised at the crow's expense. A tapeworm takes a dog's food from inside its gut. Mistletoe pushes roots into a tree's pipes and drinks. And malaria: a single-celled parasite, carried by a mosquito, that enters red blood cells, eats their haemoglobin, multiplies, and bursts them in waves that bring the fever. It is why the sickle allele is common, from VIII.4 L04.

The lines between the three blur. A commensal can become a parasite when its host is weak; a parasite that kills its host quickly does worse than one that keeps it alive; and some mutualists began as parasites that became useful. The ledger is measured, not assumed.

**Commensalism: one gains, the other is unaffected. Parasitism: one gains, the other pays. Mutualism: both gain. Which is which is decided by measuring each partner with and without the other.**
