A reaction whose free energy falls from where it stands is exergonic: it runs by itself, toward the valley. One whose free energy would rise is endergonic: it does not run by itself. It runs backwards instead, also toward the valley.

The same reaction can be either, depending on where you start and how hot it is. Hydrogen and oxygen making water is exergonic almost everywhere. Water splitting is endergonic almost everywhere, and only becomes exergonic where the temperature is so high that the gain in ways outweighs the heat cost.

The valley floor is dynamic equilibrium seen on the Hill. At the floor, forward and backward run at equal rates, and the free energy is as low as this mix can go.

For burning, the floor sits so far toward the products that, in practice, everything burns and nothing un-burns. For gas dissolving, the floor sits in the middle, and it slides toward the gas as you warm the water, which was the warm fizzy bottle.

**Exergonic: free energy falls, runs by itself. Endergonic: free energy rises, does not. Every reaction runs downhill to its valley floor, and stops there.**

Two more words, said once. A reaction that gives out heat is exothermic; one that runs by itself is exergonic. They usually agree, and sometimes, as with ice melting, they do not.
