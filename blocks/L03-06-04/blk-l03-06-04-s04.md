At the start, twenty on the left and none on the right: lots of crossings left-to-right, none the other way. The right room fills. As it fills, more people cross back. The left-to-right rate falls as the left empties; the right-to-left rate rises as the right fills.

They meet. Ten and ten, and now the two rates are equal. The counts stop changing.

But the tagged person keeps walking. Through the door, back, through again. Everyone is still moving. The rooms look still because for every person going one way, one is going the other.

A reaction that can run both ways is reversible. Gas dissolving in the bottle and gas escaping from the water are one reversible reaction. When the two directions run at equal rates, so that nothing changes on the outside while everything keeps moving on the inside, the reaction is at dynamic equilibrium.

Where the balance settles does not depend on where you start. Twenty on the left or twenty on the right, it ends at the same split. That split is set by how easy the door is each way, which is to say, by the Hill.

**At equilibrium the reaction has not stopped. It runs both ways at the same rate, so the amounts stand still while the molecules do not.**
