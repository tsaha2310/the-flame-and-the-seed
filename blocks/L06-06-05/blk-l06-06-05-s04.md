A cell's tape is far larger than what it uses at any moment. Copying all of it costs more coins than the cell has, and puts proteins where they harm. Switches keep most genes off, and a cell type is the set that is on.

And a switch must be able to flip. A repressor that cannot feel lactose is a mutation, a change in the tape, that leaves the switch stuck. The gene is intact; the lock has no keyhole. The cell cannot respond to its world. Mutations in switches, in Book VIII, turn out to be a large part of how living things change.

**All genes on: bankruptcy and chaos. A switch that cannot flip: a recipe the cell can never open. A genome is recipes and switches, and a switch must respond.**
