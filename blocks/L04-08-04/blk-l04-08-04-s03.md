The two strands run in opposite directions. Where one runs five-prime to three-prime, its partner runs three-prime to five-prime. The pairs only fit that way round, because of the angle the bases make with their backbones. Strands that run opposite ways are antiparallel.

So the partner of ATGGCA, written in its own five-prime to three-prime direction, is TGCCAT: swap every letter for its pair, then reverse. Not TACCGT.

A strand you can build a partner from is a template. Give the top strand to a builder, and pairing decides every letter of the bottom. The bottom strand is a template for the top, in the same way. Each strand holds the recipe for the other. That is what will make copying possible, in Book VIII.

And the zip: the A-T rich helix opens at a lower temperature than the G-C rich one. Two hydrogen bonds per rung let go before three do. The Crowd from II.4 said melting follows the pulls, and it does here, rung by rung.
