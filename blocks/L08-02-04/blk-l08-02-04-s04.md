Under blending, variation shrinks by half each generation. A population becomes uniform, and a rare version, however useful, is diluted to nothing before it can spread. A theory that says the useful version spreads cannot work in a blending world. Darwin's critics said so, and he had no answer.

Under Mendel's particles, factors pass whole. The recessive short factor hid in every hybrid for a generation and came back unchanged. A useful version can hide, spread and reappear. Variation does not decay; it is shuffled. Selection, in VIII.5, has something lasting to work on.

Mendel's paper sat unread in libraries while Darwin wrestled with this. When it was rediscovered in 1900, the two ideas fitted together within a few decades, and the fit is the foundation of all of biology since.

**Blending inheritance halves variation every generation and erases anything rare. Particle inheritance keeps factors whole, so variation persists and a rare useful version can spread. Mendel's peas rescued Darwin.**
