Proteins contain sulphur, in two of the twenty amino acids from IV.5. DNA contains none. DNA contains phosphorus, in every nucleotide's phosphate from IV.6. Proteins contain almost none. So sulphur labels the coat and phosphorus labels the core, and never the other way.

A virus that infects bacteria is a bacteriophage, "bacteria-eater". It attaches to the cell's surface by its tail and injects.

Sulphur-labelled viruses: after blending and spinning, the glow is in the liquid, with the knocked-off coats. Almost none is inside the bacteria. The protein stayed outside.

Phosphorus-labelled viruses: the glow is in the pellet, inside the bacteria. The DNA went in. And when the bacteria burst, the new viruses carry some of the phosphorus label. The injected DNA was used to build the next generation.

**Label the protein with sulphur and it stays outside. Label the DNA with phosphorus and it goes in and comes out in the children. The instructions are DNA.**

This was the fourth isotope case in the course, and the one that ended the argument. After 1952, nobody said DNA was boring.
