The tree gained 74 kilograms. The soil lost 57 grams. Van Helmont was right that the tree does not eat soil, and wrong about what it does eat. He said water, because water was all he had added. A litre of water is a kilogram, and he had added many.

Two hundred years later the balance was made to close. A tree is mostly carbon, from Book IV's ladder, and the carbon comes from the air, as carbon dioxide, a gas so thin that a tree must take it from thousands of cubic metres of air. The hydrogen comes from water. The oxygen in the tree's sugar comes from carbon dioxide too. The oxygen the tree gives off comes from the water; that is a lesson for itself.

Sealed with its starting air, the tree in the sim grows a few grams and stops. It has used the carbon dioxide in the box and there is no more.

This is the reverse of Book IV's ash lesson: carbon dioxide and water, the bottom of the Hill, pushed back up into sugar. It is endergonic, from III.8, and it costs exactly what burning the sugar gives back. Something must push. That something is the module.

**A tree's mass comes from the air. Carbon dioxide and water are pushed back up the Hill into sugar, and the push is light.**
