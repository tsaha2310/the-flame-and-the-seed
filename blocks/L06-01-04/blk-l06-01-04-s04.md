Three things a tape must do. Carry information: both molecules can. Be copied exactly: DNA can, because each strand is a template for the other, from IV.8; a protein's fold has no template. Survive: DNA lasts years dry, from IV.8 L02; a protein's information is in its fold, and folds drift, unfold and tangle.

The protein world fails the last two. Its labels go the wrong way, its transformations do not happen, and its seeds die in the drawer.

Twenty beads is a richer alphabet than four letters, but the tape's job is not to be rich. It is to be copied and to last. Four letters that pair and a backbone with no weak spot won.

**A tape must be copyable and durable. DNA's paired strands copy and its missing oxygen lasts. Protein does neither, which is why the instructions are DNA and the machines are protein.**
