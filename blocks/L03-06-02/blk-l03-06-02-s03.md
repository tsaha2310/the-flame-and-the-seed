To go from fuel and oxygen to carbon dioxide and water, bonds must break first, and breaking costs. Only after that do the new bonds form and pay back more than was spent. So the path from reactants to products goes up before it goes down. That rise is the hump.

The height of the hump is the activation energy: the energy a collision must bring to get over it. A collision softer than that bounces back. Only the hardest collisions in the crowd cross.

At room temperature, the collisions between methane and oxygen are almost never that hard. Nothing crosses. The gas waits. A match heats a small patch to a thousand degrees; there, most collisions cross. The crossing releases heat, which heats the next patch. The flame walks along on its own heat, and the match is no longer needed.

**A downhill reaction still has to climb a hump first. The hump is why fuel waits for a match, and why the match is only needed once.**

Warming raises the rate steeply because the share of collisions hard enough to cross grows very fast with temperature. That was the milk.
