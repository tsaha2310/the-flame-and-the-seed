Kabir watches the cheaters win and then drown in their own success.

"They win until there are too many of them."

"Every cheater's problem," says Ajji. "Book VIII has a whole lesson on it."

**Predict first:** for each, who wins over twenty generations?

<!-- STEP id="run" -->
**Turn the dial.** Threshold of one: every cell glows alone from the first division, spending coins on invisible light; the colony grows slower than a normal one and is out-competed within days. Cheaters: they read the signal, never pay for slime or glow, and divide faster; their share climbs generation by generation; when they are most of the colony, there is too little slime and the biofilm falls apart in the flow, cheaters and all. The jammer: the cells never reach threshold, never build slime, and stay as loners; the body's macrophages, from V.3, eat them one by one; the wound clears. But the sim adds a line: a mutation that changes the pocket by one letter makes a switch the jammer no longer fits, and that strain spreads.

<!-- STEP id="nails" -->
**What this nails:** Quorum sensing is a bargain that cheaters can break and jammers can exploit. Breaking a bacterial conversation is a real medical idea, and, like every drug, it faces the mutation problem of Book VIII.
