The dam. The short staircase between the two towers pumped hydrogen ions into the disc. Splitting water added four more per oxygen. The disc is now crowded with ions and positive, and the space outside the disc is not. The same ATP synthase as the mitochondrion's sits in the disc's membrane, facing outward. Ions flow out through it; it spins; it presses ADP and phosphate into ATP. Light has filled a dam, and the dam has made coins.

So the light reactions end with two things in the chloroplast's open space: NADPH, electrons held high, and ATP, coins. Neither is sugar. Both are what the sugar-making will spend.

**The oxygen a leaf gives off comes from water, proven with labelled atoms. The light-filled dam spins the same turbine as the mitochondrion and makes ATP. Light reactions end with NADPH and ATP, not sugar.**
