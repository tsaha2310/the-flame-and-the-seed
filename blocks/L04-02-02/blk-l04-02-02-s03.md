C4H10 has two arrangements. C5H12 has three. C6H14 has five. C10H22 has seventy-five. Each arrangement is a different molecule with its own boiling point and its own smell. Molecules with the same formula and different skeletons are isomers.

Two carbons with four hydrogens cannot be a chain of single bonds; there are not enough hydrogens. The carbons must share two pairs: a double bond. A hydrocarbon with a double bond is an alkene. Ethene, the two-carbon one, is the gas that ripens bananas.

Two carbons with two hydrogens must share three pairs: a triple bond. That is an alkyne. Ethyne is the gas in a welding torch.

A double bond does something a single bond does not: it locks. Two carbons joined by a single bond can spin freely around it, like beads on a string. Joined by a double bond, they cannot. The molecule has a fixed kink at that point. Tomorrow's module hangs on that kink.

**Same atoms can be joined in different arrangements. Each arrangement is a different molecule, an isomer. Double and triple bonds are two more ways to be different.**
