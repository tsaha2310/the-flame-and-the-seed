The copyist is an enzyme, RNA polymerase. It attaches to the DNA at the start of a gene, unzips a short stretch of the helix, from IV.8's zipper, and reads one strand, the template. Against each DNA letter it adds the pairing RNA nucleotide: A across from T, U across from A, G across from C, C across from G, joined by the condensation from IV.7. It moves along, unzipping ahead and zipping shut behind, and a single strand of RNA peels off. At the gene's end it lets go.

Copying a gene from DNA into RNA is transcription: writing across. The copy is a messenger RNA, mRNA, because it carries the message to the workshop.

The master is untouched. The helix has zipped shut behind the copyist, every letter in place. Ten copies can be made in a row, and a hundred copyists can work on different genes at once.

And the copies are short-lived. RNA cuts its own backbone within hours, from IV.8 L02. In the sim, each copy fades. That is not a flaw. Tomorrow's lesson is about why.

**Transcription: RNA polymerase reads one strand of a gene and writes a single-strand mRNA copy by pairing. The master stays in the nucleus; the copy goes out, is used, and wears out.**
