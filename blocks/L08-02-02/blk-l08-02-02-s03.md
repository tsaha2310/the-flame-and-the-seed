Each plant carries two factors for a trait. A pure tall plant has two tall factors, TT. A pure short has tt. Each parent gives one factor, chosen at random, to each child.

TT crossed with tt: every child gets a T and a t, so every child is Tt. Every child is tall, because one tall factor is enough. The version that shows whenever it is present is dominant. The version that hides behind it is recessive. Dominant only means it shows in a hybrid. It does not mean common, or strong, or better.

Tt crossed with Tt: each parent gives T or t at random, like a coin. The square has four boxes: TT, Tt, tT, tt. Three of the four have at least one T and are tall. One is tt and is short. Three to one. The short factor was in every hybrid, hidden; it shows only when a child gets one from each side.

The square of all combinations is a Punnett square. It is III.8's counting of ways, done for two coins, one per parent.

**Each plant carries two factors per trait and gives one to each child at random. A dominant version shows with one copy; a recessive version shows only with two. The square counts the ways: three to one.**

Mendel called them factors. Nobody knew what a factor was made of. He died in 1884 with his paper unread; it was rediscovered in 1900, and Book VI has since said what a factor is.
