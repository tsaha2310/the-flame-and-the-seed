"In 1770," says Ajji, "every chemist knew why things burn. They were wrong, and they were not stupid."

Here is what they believed. Everything that can burn contains a fire-stuff called phlogiston. When it burns, the phlogiston flies out. Wood is rich in it. Ash is wood with the phlogiston gone.

"It explains a lot," Ajji says. "Ash is lighter than wood. A flame in a jar goes out, because the air fills up with phlogiston and cannot take any more. Charcoal burns almost completely, so it must be nearly pure phlogiston."

Kabir is nodding. It is a good story.
