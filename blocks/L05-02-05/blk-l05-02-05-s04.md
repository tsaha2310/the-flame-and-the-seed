Water only: nothing reaches the blood but a trickle. There is no salt gradient to follow. Salt only: sodium ions are stopped by the wall, the way V.2 L01 showed; with the cholera-jammed channels running the other way, almost none gets in. Sugar only: glucose's own carrier is slow and cannot keep up.

Salt and sugar together, in the right amounts: a carrier on the gut side of the cell binds one sodium and one glucose at once, and only flips when both are in. Sodium is far more crowded in the gut than in the cell, because the pump keeps the cell's sodium low. Sodium falling into the cell is a fall, and the carrier couples that fall to dragging glucose in, from III.8. Glucose is lifted into the cell on sodium's back. Then both pass out into the blood, and water follows the salt, through water channels, litres of it. That is the secret of ORS: sodium drags glucose in, and water follows both.

Double the sugar, and the gut is now sweeter than the blood. Water goes the wrong way, out into the gut, by osmosis. The patient gets worse. The recipe is a concentration, from III.5, not a dose.

**Sodium falling into the cell, down the gradient the pump built, drags glucose in with it, and water follows the salt. That is why ORS needs both salt and sugar, in exact amounts.**

Dilip Mahalanabis did not know the carrier. He knew the recipe worked, from experiments in Kolkata a few years before, and he had the courage to use it on thousands when the drips ran out. The carrier was found later. The recipe has saved more than fifty million lives.
