Plants capture light and lift carbon into sugar, from V.10. Everything else eats. A living thing that makes its own food from light is a producer. One that gets its food by eating others is a consumer. Plant-eaters are consumers; so are the things that eat them; so is the mongoose.

At every arrow, energy is lost. A caterpillar that eats a leaf burns most of the leaf's sugar on the staircase, from V.6 and V.7, and the burned part leaves as heat. A third of the fall caught, from V.6 L01, and much of that spent on living. Only about a tenth of the energy in the leaf ends up as caterpillar that a sparrow can eat. And a tenth of the sparrow ends up as cat. Energy flows one way through the garden, from light to heat, and thins tenfold at each step. That is coupling's waste, from III.8, seen at the scale of a garden. Nothing recycles energy; the Levelling takes it.

So chains are short. A tenth of a tenth of a tenth leaves almost nothing by the fourth or fifth step, and a chain of seven cannot feed its top. That is why big predators are rare and why there is no animal that eats tigers for a living.

The arrows in a real garden cross and rejoin: a sparrow eats caterpillars and seeds, a frog eats grasshoppers and aphids. The whole tangle of who eats whom is a food web.

**Producers capture light; consumers eat. Energy flows one way along the arrows and thins about tenfold at each step, because every eater burns most of its meal. Chains are short, and the tangle of them is a food web.**
