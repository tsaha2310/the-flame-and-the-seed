A group of similar cells doing one job together is a tissue. The leaf has several, each a switch setting from VI.6 held by a group.

The skin, one cell thick with a wax coat, keeps water in. Its pore cells, in pairs, swell and shrink by turgor, from V.2 L04, to open and close a hole for carbon dioxide in and oxygen out. The palisade cells, packed with chloroplasts, make sugar. The spongy cells leave air gaps so gas can reach the palisade.

The veins are two pipes. The water pipe is xylem: dead cells, walls only, stacked end to end into tubes that carry water and minerals up from the root by the pull of evaporation from the leaves. The sugar pipe is phloem: living cells that load the sugar the leaf makes, from V.10, and carry it to the root, the flowers and the growing tips.

At each tip, a small group of cells keeps dividing, from V.1's cell theory, and every cell in the plant comes from one of these groups. A dividing tip is a meristem. Cells born there take on jobs as they are pushed away from it, by the signals of their neighbours.

A green cell in the root makes no sugar in the dark and wastes its chloroplasts. Wrong page for that address.

**A tissue is a group of cells sharing a job. A leaf is skin, pores, green cells and pipes: xylem for water up, phloem for sugar down. Meristems at the tips make every new cell.**
