Kabir's one-way genome makes twenty thousand proteins and no variants. He lets the splicing vary and the count climbs.

**Predict first:** how many proteins does the genome make, and what does the cell lose?

<!-- STEP id="run" -->
**Turn the dial.** The protein count falls to about the gene count. Every cell makes the same version of every protein. The sim's plant cannot make the membrane version of a protein in one tissue and the free version in another, and its leaf and root end up more alike than they should be. It lives, and it is poorer.

**What this nails:** Alternative splicing multiplies what a tape can spell without lengthening it. Book VII's tissues use it constantly.
