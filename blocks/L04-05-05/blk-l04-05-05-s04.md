The three outcomes of a swap.

A buried oily bead swapped for a charge: the fold breaks. The protein cannot bury a charge, refolds some other way, and loses its pocket. It does nothing.

A surface polar bead swapped for a different polar bead: nothing happens. The fold is the same, the surface is still wet, the pocket still fits. Most swaps are like this.

A surface polar bead swapped for an oily one: the fold holds, the pocket works, and the protein has grown a sticky patch. In a crowd, the patches find each other. The protein works alone and fails in numbers.

So the sequence is not just "mostly right is fine". Some positions can change freely. Some cannot change at all. And the difference is where the bead sits in the fold, which the sequence itself decides.

**One bead in three hundred can break a fold, do nothing, or turn a working protein sticky. Which one depends on where in the fold that bead sits.**
