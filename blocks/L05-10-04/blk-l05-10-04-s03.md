Lamp always on: the plant makes oxygen and sugar all day, the snail breathes the oxygen and makes carbon dioxide, the plant takes the carbon dioxide. Both live. The gases hold steady.

Lamp always off: the plant makes nothing and burns its store. The snail breathes. Oxygen falls, carbon dioxide rises, and both die when the oxygen is gone.

Twelve-hour cycle: by day the plant makes more oxygen than both of them use. By night the plant makes none and burns sugar, exactly as the snail does. Oxygen falls all night and recovers by day. Both live, if the plant is big enough for the snail.

Take the snail out, and at midnight the oxygen is still falling. The plant is breathing. Plants respire all day and all night. Photosynthesis, by day, is on top of that, and bigger, which is why a plant is a net oxygen-maker over a day. But the powerhouse in every plant cell never stops, because a leaf's own coins come from burning, not from light.

Now the two equations from III.1, side by side. Sugar plus oxygen gives carbon dioxide and water, downhill, in every cell. Carbon dioxide and water plus light give sugar and oxygen, uphill, in the green ones. One is the other reversed. What one makes the other uses. Together they close a loop, and the jar is the loop in a bottle.

**Respiration and photosynthesis are two halves of one loop. Plants respire all day and all night; by day they photosynthesise on top of it. The jar lives if the halves balance.**
