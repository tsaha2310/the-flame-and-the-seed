A sodium next to a sodium is thrown off the board. A sodium next to a chloride snaps in. Every ion sits with six of the opposite kind around it: front, back, left, right, above, below.

The pull between a positive ion and a negative one, when they hold each other this way, is called an ionic bond. It is not a bond between one pair. Each ion is held by all six neighbours, and they by theirs, out to the edge of the crystal.

The whole arrangement is a lattice: a repeating pattern in all three directions. The pattern here is a cube, which is why the tiny grains in the saucer are cubes, and why rock salt breaks into cubes under the hammer.

Sodium chloride is not "a molecule of salt". There is no such thing. A grain of salt is one enormous lattice, and its recipe, one sodium to one chloride, is just the ratio that keeps the charges balanced.

**An ionic solid is one giant checkerboard of opposite charges, each ion held by all its neighbours.**

That is why it takes a very hot flame to melt salt, and why it shatters rather than bends.
