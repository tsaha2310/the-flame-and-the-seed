Bring a carboxyl's O-H to a hydroxyl's O-H. The carboxyl keeps one oxygen, the hydroxyl keeps its oxygen, and the two spare pieces, an O-H from one and an H from the other, leave together as a water molecule. The chain and the glycerol are now joined through an oxygen. That link is called an ester.

A long hydrocarbon chain with a carboxyl on the end is a fatty acid. Twelve to eighteen carbons is usual. The acid end is the only polar spot on it; the rest is fuel, from IV.2.

Glycerol has three hydroxyls, so it can take three fatty acids. The result, three tails on one glycerol, is a triglyceride. Ghee, mustard oil, the oil in the diya, the fat in a peanut, the fat stored in the moong seed for its sprout: all triglycerides.

Three water molecules leave when a fat is built. And, when a fat is digested, three water molecules go back in and the ester links come apart. Joining by losing water, and splitting by adding it back, will turn out to be the way every long molecule in the seed is built. This is its first appearance.

**A fat is glycerol with three fatty acid tails joined by ester links, and each link was made by squeezing out one water molecule.**
