The protein sits on a short site of DNA just past the promoter, gripping it by shape. With it there, the copyist can land on the promoter but cannot move forward; the road is blocked. No copy, no enzyme. The protein is a repressor: it presses the gene down.

Lactose arrives. It fits the repressor's second pocket. The fit bends the fold, from V.4's allostery, and the bent repressor no longer fits the DNA. It lets go. The copyist moves, the three genes are copied together on one message, and the cutters are made. Lactose is digested. When it is gone, the repressor straightens, grips the site again, and the gene goes quiet.

The three genes and their shared promoter and switch site, run as one unit, are an operon. This one is the lac operon, worked out by Jacob and Monod in Paris in 1961. It was the first switch anyone understood, and its logic, a repressor that the signal itself unbends, is used everywhere.

**A repressor is an allosteric protein that grips the DNA after a promoter and blocks the copyist. The thing to be digested fits its second pocket, bends it off the DNA, and the gene switches on.**

The bacterium does not know lactose is there. Lactose bumps into repressors, from the Crowd, and shape decides. Nobody is in charge.
