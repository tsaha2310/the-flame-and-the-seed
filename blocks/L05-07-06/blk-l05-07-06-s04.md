Fat is carbon and hydrogen with a little oxygen, from IV.3. Every carbon leaves the body as carbon dioxide, breathed out: acetyl-CoA into the wheel, the wheel strips it, the lungs release it. Every hydrogen ends up on oxygen as water, at the bottom of the chain, and leaves as urine, sweat and breath moisture.

Ten kilograms of fat need about twenty-nine kilograms of oxygen to burn. Out come about twenty-eight kilograms of carbon dioxide and eleven of water. The balance from Book I closes to the gram.

More than four-fifths of the mass leaves through the lungs. The fat is breathed out. Most doctors, asked this question in a survey, got it wrong; "energy" was the most common answer. Energy is not stuff. The fall from the Hill left as heat and work. The atoms left as gas.

**Lost fat leaves as carbon dioxide from the lungs and water. Mass is conserved; the atoms are breathed out. Energy is what fell, not what left.**

The labelled oxygen answers the last piece: breathed-in oxygen leaves almost entirely as water, not as carbon dioxide. The carbon dioxide's oxygens were already on the fat and on the water the wheel added. Isotopes, from Book I, are how anyone knows.
