One step: the whole thirty-coin fall lands on one wheel. A wheel can lift one coin's worth, from IV.6 L04. One ATP is recharged; twenty-nine coins' worth become heat in an instant. The cell's water climbs past 60 degrees. Every enzyme unfolds, from V.4. The cell is the boiled seed. This is the flame, done inside a wall: a short circuit.

Two steps: two coins, and still enough heat to cook.

Ten steps: ten coins, and the heat comes in ten small pieces, each carried away by the water before the next.

Thirty steps: about thirty coins, each step a fall about the size of one ATP, each coupled to its own wheel, the heat spread thin. The cell stays at 37. This is what the seed does. It is the diya's fall, broken into thirty small falls, each caught.

The name for burning glucose in small steps, inside a cell, with the falls caught as ATP, is cellular respiration. It is the same equation as the flame, and it is the seed's whole difference from the flame in one word: steps.

**The seed does not burn glucose in one drop. It walks the fall down a staircase of small steps, each with a wheel, and catches about thirty coins. One drop would cook it. The flame takes the drop.**

There is a ceiling. Past thirty or so steps, each step is too small to recharge a coin, and nothing more is gained. The real staircase has about that many.
