Carbon and hydrogen pull almost the same. The cloud sits centred. A fair share.

Oxygen pulls much harder than hydrogen. The cloud leans toward the oxygen. Not stolen; the hydrogen still has a share. But the oxygen end of the bond has more of the electrons than its fair half, and the hydrogen end has less.

More electrons at one end means that end is slightly negative. Fewer at the other means slightly positive. Not a full charge like an ion. A fraction. This is a partial charge.

A bond with partial charges at its ends is a polar bond. Polar means "having two poles", like a magnet or the Earth. The bond between oxygen and hydrogen in water is strongly polar. The bond between nitrogen and hydrogen in ammonia is polar. The bond between carbon and hydrogen in oil is not.

**When two atoms share unequally, the bond gets a slightly negative end and a slightly positive end. That is a polar bond.**
