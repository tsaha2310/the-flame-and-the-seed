The rains are over. The sky is a hard blue. The verandah is dry, and the brass diya sits cold on its shelf with a black wick.

The moong plant from the first night has been compost for months. Its seeds grew, and their seeds grew, and the jar on the shelf holds six generations. Kabir's plant, the one he named, has pods.

Ajji puts the notebook on the table, and beside it the torn page, folded small, that she has kept in her purse all along.

"Not yet," she says, when Kabir reaches for it. "First, build one."
