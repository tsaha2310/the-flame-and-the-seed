The steal is done. Sodium's electron falls into chlorine's outer shell, both reach eight, and the charged pair sit low on the Hill. Millions of them line up, and you get a cube you can put on your dal.

But what if neither atom is much stronger than the other in the Tug-of-War? Two chlorines, say. Neither wins.

"Then they share," says Kabir. "Like the dupatta when Rahul got tired."

He is right, and it is the grip that makes water, sugar, oil, and almost the whole seed.
