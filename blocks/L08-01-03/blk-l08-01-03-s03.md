A cell's life runs in a loop: grow, copy the tape, grow a little more, divide, and start again. That loop is the cell cycle. Most of it is growing and copying. The division itself is short, and it is the dance.

First, replication: every chromosome is copied, and the two copies stay joined at one point. Then each chromosome winds itself tight, from VI.1 L03's coiling, so that two metres of thread becomes a few short rods that can be moved without tangling. The nuclear envelope, from VI.5, dissolves. Threads of protein grow from the two ends of the cell and attach to each chromosome's joined point, and the chromosomes are pulled to the middle and lined up. Then the join is cut, and walking proteins, from V.5, haul one copy of each to each end. Two envelopes form around the two sets. The cell pinches in two, or, in a plant, builds a wall across the middle.

The dance from winding to two nuclei is mitosis. Each daughter has exactly the parent's chromosomes: twenty-two in a moong, forty-six in Kabir. Same tape, whole, in both.

**The cell cycle is grow, copy, divide. Mitosis is the dance: wind the copied chromosomes tight, dissolve the envelope, line them up, pull one copy of each to each end, rebuild two nuclei, split. Two daughters, each with the full tape.**
