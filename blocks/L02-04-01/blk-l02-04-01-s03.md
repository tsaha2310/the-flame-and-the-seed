Temperature is how hard the particles jiggle. Warm means fast; cold means slow. It is the average kinetic energy of the crowd, and nothing more mysterious than that.

Stickiness is how hard the particles pull on each other. It is the hydrogen bonds, dipole forces and dispersion forces from II.3, all in one dial.

The state of a thing is a contest between the two.

- Stickiness wins outright: the particles jiggle in place and cannot leave their neighbours. A solid.
- Jiggle is enough to let them slide but not escape: a liquid.
- Jiggle wins outright: they fly apart and fill any box. A gas.

The temperature at which a solid loses the contest and becomes a liquid is its melting point. The temperature at which the liquid loses and becomes a gas is its boiling point. Stickier stuff has higher ones, because it takes more jiggle to break free.

This box, with its two dials, is the Crowd. It joins the Hill and the Tug-of-War on the wall.

**A state of matter is a contest between jiggle and stickiness. Temperature is the jiggle. The forces between molecules are the stickiness.**

Ghee's stickiness is small, dispersion only. A warm roti gives its molecules enough jiggle to slide. A cool draught takes it away again.
