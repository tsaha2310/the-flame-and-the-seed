Carbon dioxide is carbon that has fallen as far as it can. Water is hydrogen that has fallen as far as it can. Sand is silicon at the bottom. Rust is iron at the bottom. These are not fuels; they are what fuels become. They sit on the valley floor of the Hill.

To make a fuel from them, something must push the carbon back up the scoreboard: take its oxygens away and give it hydrogens. That is endergonic. It costs free energy, exactly as much as the burn gave out, plus waste.

The push-back panel does it with a plain arrow labelled energy in. Carbon dioxide and water go in; a sugar comes out, with its carbons at zero; oxygen is released. Where that energy comes from in a leaf, and what the wheel is, is Book V. But the direction is clear now, and so is the cost.

**Carbon dioxide and water are the bottom of the Hill: carbon and hydrogen with nothing left to lose. Making fuel from them means pushing them back up, and that costs.**

The whole living world runs between these two: something pushes carbon up into sugar, and everything else lets it fall back down.
