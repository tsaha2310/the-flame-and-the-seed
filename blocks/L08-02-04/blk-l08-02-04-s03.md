Kabir watches the paint world turn grey.

"Everything becomes average. Nothing new can survive."

"That was the headache. A useful new trait, under blending, is halved in every child and gone in five generations. Darwin could not see how anything could ever change."

**Predict first:** what happens to the range of shades after fifty generations of blending?

<!-- STEP id="run" -->
**Turn the dial.** In the paint world, every child is the average of two parents, so extremes vanish. The range halves every generation. By fifty, the whole population is one middle grey, and stays there. A single dark plant in a light population is diluted to a quarter-shade in two generations and gone in five; it can never spread. In the particle world, the factors pass whole. The range stays as wide at fifty generations as at the start. The one dark plant's factor is carried, hidden or shown, into more and more descendants, and if dark plants do better, from tomorrow's module, they can take over.

<!-- STEP id="nails" -->
**What this nails:** Blending destroys variation; particles preserve it. Darwin's theory needs variation to persist so that selection can act on it. Mendel's peas rescued Darwin, though neither man knew it.
