Two rubbed balloons push each other away. A rubbed balloon and a rubbed glass rod pull toward each other. A rubbed comb behaves like the balloon. Silk behaves like the glass.

There are exactly two kinds. Not three, not one. Every rubbed thing in the lab is one kind or the other.

The property rubbing gives an object is called charge. The two kinds are called positive and negative. Nothing about the names is special; they could be "red" and "blue". What matters is the rule.

- Like charges repel: they push apart.
- Opposite charges attract: they pull together.
- An unrubbed object has both kinds in equal amounts, so it does neither, though a strongly charged object can still tug it a little.

**There are two kinds of charge. Same kinds push apart. Different kinds pull together.**

Rubbing does not make charge. It moves a little from one object to the other, so the balloon ends up with extra negative and Kabir's hair with extra positive. That is why his hair stands up and reaches for the balloon.
