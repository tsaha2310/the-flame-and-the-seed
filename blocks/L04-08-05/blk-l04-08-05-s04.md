The backbones of the two strands are a fixed distance apart, set by the sugar and phosphate. A rung has to span that gap. A big base plus a small base spans it exactly, and the edge groups meet at the right distance for hydrogen bonds. That is A-T and G-C.

Big plus big is too long: the rails bulge. Small plus small is too short: the bases cannot touch, and the rails pinch. Neither can make proper hydrogen bonds, and both leave a mark on the helix's shape.

So the letters can be in any order, and the helix stays the same width, because every position holds exactly one of the two acceptable pairs. Pairing is not just choosy; it is size-matched. The four bases are two shapes of a single width.

**Every rung is one big base and one small, which is why the helix is the same width everywhere. A wrong pair bulges or pinches, and can be felt.**
