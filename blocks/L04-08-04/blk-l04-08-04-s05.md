Kabir's parallel helix will not zip. He sets the arrows opposite and the rungs click into place.

**Predict first:** do the pairs still form?

<!-- STEP id="run" -->
**Turn the dial.** With parallel strands, the bases meet at the wrong angle. A and T can no longer line up their givers and takers; G and C manage one bond instead of three. The helix cannot form a regular spiral; it kinks and gapes, and the zip will not close.

<!-- STEP id="nails" -->
**What this nails:** Antiparallel is not a convention. It is the only way the pairs fit, and every copying and reading machine in Books VI and VIII is built to run along a strand backwards relative to its partner.
