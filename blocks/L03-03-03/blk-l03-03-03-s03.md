The score each atom ends with is its oxidation number. It says: if every shared pair went wholly to the stronger puller, how many electrons would this atom have lost or gained?

- Lost one: plus 1. Hydrogen in water.
- Gained two: minus 2. Oxygen in water.
- Tied: zero. Every atom in H2, O2, or a lump of metal.

It is bookkeeping, like the shopkeeper's ledger. Nobody in water has actually lost a whole electron. But the score lets you see a redox where no ion forms.

Hydrogen burning: H2 has hydrogen at 0. Water has hydrogen at plus 1. Hydrogen's score went up. It lost, in the ledger, and so it was oxidised. Oxygen went from 0 to minus 2. It gained, and was reduced. A redox with no ions anywhere.

**An oxidation number is a score: hand every shared pair to the stronger puller and count. A score going up is oxidation. A score going down is reduction.**

Carbon is the interesting one. In methane, four hydrogens each hand carbon a point: minus 4. In carbon dioxide, two oxygens each take two points: plus 4. Carbon can be anywhere from minus 4 to plus 4. Burning methane takes it from one end to the other.
