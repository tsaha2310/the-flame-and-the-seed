The open jar grew maggots. The cloth-covered jar grew none, but flies crawled all over the cloth trying to reach the meat. The maggots came from the flies.

Pasteur's swan-neck broth stayed clear for months. Snap the neck, and it clouded in days. Tilt the broth into the dusty bend, and it clouded too. Something in the dust was doing it.

That something is microbes: living things so small you need a lens to see one. They fall from the air. They do not appear from the broth.

The old idea has a name: spontaneous generation. Life from non-life, on its own, in a few days. It was a hypothesis, which means a guess that can be tested. It was tested. It failed.

**Life comes from life. Nothing we have ever watched has come from nowhere.**
