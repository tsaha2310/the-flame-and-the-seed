The flame delivers energy into the cup. That energy on the move, from a hot thing to a cooler thing, is called heat. Heat is not the same as temperature. Heat is the energy that flows. Temperature is how hard the particles jiggle once it has arrived.

Both cups got the same heat. The oil warmed twice as much, so the water must have soaked up the same energy while jiggling less. It takes more energy to raise a gram of water by one degree than a gram of oil.

The energy needed to raise one gram of a substance by one degree is its heat capacity. Water's is about twice oil's, about five times sand's, and higher than almost any common substance. The reason is the hydrogen bonds from II.3: some of the incoming energy goes into loosening them instead of into speeding molecules up.

**Heat is energy on the move. Temperature is the jiggle it causes. Water needs a great deal of heat to change its temperature a little.**

That is why a hot-water bag stays warm for an hour, why the sea is cooler than the sand at noon and warmer at midnight, and why Chennai, by the sea, never gets as hot or as cold as Delhi.
