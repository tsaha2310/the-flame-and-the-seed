One letter: four words. Not enough for twenty beads.

Two letters: four times four, sixteen words. Still not enough. Four beads would have no name.

Three letters: four times four times four, sixty-four words. Enough for twenty, with forty-four to spare.

Four letters: two hundred and fifty-six. Far more than needed, and every recipe would be a third longer.

So the word is three letters long. A three-letter word on the mRNA that names one amino acid is a codon. This much was worked out on paper before anyone found a single codon's meaning, from the counting rule in IV.7 L04.

The spare forty-four words are not wasted. Tomorrow and the next day are about what they do.

**A codon is three mRNA letters naming one amino acid. Three is the shortest word that gives more than twenty words: four cubed is sixty-four.**
