The three-phosphate adenine nucleotide is ATP. Snap its end phosphate off into water, and you get the two-phosphate form, ADP, plus a free phosphate, plus a fall of free energy. That fall is a coin: enough to pay for one small uphill step, joined to it by coupling, from III.8.

Spend and it is ADP. To get ATP back, something must push the phosphate back on, uphill. That is a recharge, and it costs more than the coin is worth, as III.9 said every recharge does. The charger runs on a bigger fall. In the seed, that bigger fall is sugar burning, in steps. That is Book V.

The shop has only a few hundred coins in the till at any moment. It does not save up a fortune. It spends and recharges the same coins, over and over, at a rate that would empty the till in seconds if the charger stopped.

**ATP is a coin: ADP with a third phosphate pushed on. Spend it for a small uphill step, recharge it with a bigger fall, repeat a million times a second.**

Once more, honestly: breaking the end phosphate's bond costs energy, like every bond. ATP pays out because ADP and phosphate, apart and gripped by water, sit far lower than ATP does.
