With air, the yeast grows well and uses little sugar to do it. Almost no alcohol. In the Lens, the powerhouse runs: thirty coins per glucose, and the sugar goes to gas and water.

Sealed, the yeast grows less and uses far more sugar, ten to twenty times more per gram of yeast. The flask fills with alcohol and gas. In the Lens, fermentation: two coins per glucose, and to get the same coins the yeast must gulp sugar.

Pasteur wrote it as "fermentation is life without air", and noticed the switch: give a fermenting yeast oxygen, and it stops making alcohol and sips its sugar instead. The wine-makers' sour barrels had a different microbe in them, one that turns alcohol into vinegar, with air. Keep the barrel sealed and it stays wine. That is the Pasteur effect, and the sequel to the swan neck: now the flask is sealed not to keep life out, but to keep the yeast on the path you want.

**Give yeast oxygen and it sips sugar, thirty coins each. Take oxygen away and it gulps, two coins each, and pours out alcohol. Same yeast, two pipelines, one switch.**
