Each bacterium makes a small molecule and lets it leak out through its wall. In a large volume of water the molecule drifts away and its concentration stays near zero. In the squid's small pouch, with many cells, it builds up.

The same molecule can drift back in. Inside, it fits a transcription factor, from VI.6 L04, and bends it, from allostery, into a shape that switches on the glow genes. At low concentration almost no factors are bent and the genes are off. Past a certain concentration, most are, and the genes switch on, in every cell at once.

So the concentration of the leaked molecule is a headcount, from III.5: more cells in a fixed space means more molecule per litre. A bacterium reads how many neighbours it has by reading the concentration. Sensing the number of your kind by a shared leaked molecule is quorum sensing: a quorum is the number of members needed before a meeting can act. The molecule that carries the count is a signal molecule.

**Each bacterium leaks a signal molecule and reads its concentration. Concentration is a headcount. Past a threshold, the signal bends a switch and the glow genes come on in every cell together: quorum sensing.**

The squid feeds the bacteria and flushes most of them out each dawn, so the glow starts again from a small colony each night.
