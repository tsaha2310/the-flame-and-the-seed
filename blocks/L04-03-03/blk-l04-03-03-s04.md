Straight tails lie side by side like pencils in a box. Every tail touches its neighbours along its whole length, and dispersion pulls, weak per atom but many atoms long, add up. The box locks solid at room temperature. Ghee.

A tail with a double bond has a fixed bend. Bent tails cannot lie flat against straight ones. They hold their neighbours apart, so fewer atoms touch, so the pulls are weaker. The box stays liquid at room temperature. Mustard oil.

A fatty acid with no double bonds is saturated: full up with hydrogen, every carbon holding as many as it can. One with double bonds is unsaturated: some carbons have given up a hydrogen to double-bond a neighbour.

The words on the packet mean exactly this. Saturated fat: straight tails, solid, ghee and butter. Unsaturated fat: kinked tails, liquid, most plant oils.

**A double bond locks a kink. Kinked tails cannot pack, so the fat stays liquid. Straight tails pack close, so the fat sets solid.**
