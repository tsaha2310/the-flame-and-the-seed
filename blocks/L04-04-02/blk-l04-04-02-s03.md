Two hydroxyls meet. One keeps its oxygen as a bridge; the other gives up its whole O-H, and the first gives up its H, and the two pieces leave as water. The rings are now joined through one oxygen. Same move as the ester in IV.3.

Joining two molecules by squeezing out a water is called condensation. Two sugar rings joined this way make a disaccharide, a two-sugar.

- Glucose plus glucose: maltose, malt sugar, the taste of the chewed roti.
- Glucose plus fructose: sucrose, table sugar, from cane.
- Glucose plus galactose: lactose, milk sugar.

Add the water back at the link and the two rings come apart. Splitting a molecule by putting a water in is called hydrolysis: water-splitting. Your gut hydrolyses sucrose before it can absorb it. Some people's guts cannot hydrolyse lactose, and milk troubles them.

**Life's molecules are joined by condensation, squeezing out a water, and taken apart by hydrolysis, putting one back. Fats, sugars, and every long molecule to come.**

The move is the same on every rung of the ladder. Learn it here and you have learned how the seed builds and breaks everything.
