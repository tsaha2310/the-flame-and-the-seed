Bend a strand so that a stretch of letters lies against a stretch that matches it, in reverse, and the pairs form: A with U, G with C, along the two arms. The arms zip together and the letters between them become a loop at the top. That shape is a hairpin.

A long strand can make several hairpins. It folds into a shape, the same shape every time, set by its sequence, exactly as a protein's fold is set by its beads. The seventy-six-letter strand always folds into the clover leaf, because that is where its pairs are.

And a folded RNA has a shape, and shape is a job. Some RNAs are tools: they carry an amino acid on one arm, they hold other molecules in a pocket, and, as Book VI shows, one of them does the most important cutting-and-joining in the seed.

So the same strand does two things. Read along it, and it is a message: letters in order. Fold it, and it is a machine: a shape. A strip of letters that can be copied, read, edited and passed on, and that folds into a tool, is the fifth model: the Tape. It joins the Hill, the Tug-of-War, the Crowd and Lock and Shape on the wall.

**A strand folds back on itself wherever its letters can pair, into hairpins and loops. Its sequence sets its shape. RNA is both a message and a machine, and that is the Tape.**
