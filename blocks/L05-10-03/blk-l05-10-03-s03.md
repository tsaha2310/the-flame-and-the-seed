At two seconds, one spot: a three-carbon piece with a phosphate. The very first thing the labelled carbon becomes is the split product from RuBisCO's pocket.

At five seconds, G3P carries the label. At ten, the five-carbon acceptor does, which means the label has gone round the wheel and into the rebuild. At thirty, sucrose and the starts of amino acids. At sixty, starch.

The order of the spots is the order of the wheel. Calvin and his students spent ten years on this, finding each spot, naming each enzyme, and closing the cycle. It earned him the Nobel Prize in 1961, and the wheel his name.

Lights off: the label stops entering, and the wheel stops within a minute, because ATP and NADPH are no longer being filled. The Calvin cycle does not use light directly. It uses what light made, and runs out of it in the dark. Every step is uphill, anabolic, from V.4 L01, and every step is paid for.

**Calvin fed algae labelled carbon dioxide and froze them seconds later. The label appeared in a three-carbon piece first, then G3P, then the acceptor, then sugars. The order of appearance is the order of the wheel.**
