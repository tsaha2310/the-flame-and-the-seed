Nitrogen in ammonia has four groups of clouds, not three. Three are shared with hydrogens. The fourth is a pair that belongs to nitrogen alone. A pair like that is called a lone pair.

Four groups push apart into a tetrahedron. But you only see three of the corners, because the fourth has no atom on it. The three hydrogens make a little pyramid with nitrogen at the top. The shape is called pyramidal.

Oxygen in water has four groups too: two shared, two lone. Tetrahedron again. You see two corners. The two hydrogens sit at an angle, and the shape is called bent.

The lone pairs are fatter than shared pairs, because nothing is pulling them outward to another atom. So they push the shared pairs a little closer together. Ammonia's angle is 107 degrees, not 109. Water's is about 104.

**Lone pairs take up space and push like any other pair. Count them, or the shape comes out wrong.**

Methane, four shared pairs, no lone pairs, is a true tetrahedron: 109 degrees.
