The air is four-fifths nitrogen and none of it is usable by a plant, because the two atoms share three pairs, from II.1 L03, and the hump to break them is enormous. Certain bacteria carry an enzyme that does it at soil temperature, spending a great deal of ATP, and turn nitrogen gas into ammonia the plant can build into amino acids, from IV.5. Breaking the air's nitrogen into a usable form is nitrogen fixation. The bacteria that do it inside a legume's root nodules are rhizobia. The moong, a legume, gives them sugar and a home with the oxygen kept low; they give it nitrogen. Farmers plant legumes between other crops for exactly this: the soil ends up richer than it started.

A partnership in which both sides gain is mutualism. The ledger shows it everywhere. Tree and fungus: sugar for minerals. A lichen: a fungus that holds water and a green cell that makes sugar, together living on bare rock where neither could alone. A cow and its stomach microbes: the cow chews grass and provides a warm, wet vat; the microbes cut cellulose, from IV.4, which the cow cannot, and the cow digests the microbes and their products. You and your gut microbes: you provide food and a home; they make vitamins, cut what you cannot, and crowd out invaders.

**Mutualism is a partnership where both gain. Rhizobia in a legume's nodules fix the air's nitrogen for sugar. Fungi trade minerals for sugar; a cow's microbes cut cellulose for a home. Most living things are in at least one such deal.**
