The clover leaf is a transfer RNA, tRNA, the seventy-six-letter fold from IV.7. At the bottom loop it exposes three letters that can pair with a codon: the anticodon. At the other end it has a hook where one amino acid can be attached.

The tRNA does not know which bead belongs on its hook. The charging enzyme does. It has two pockets, from Lock and Shape: one fits the tRNA's shape, including its anticodon region, and the other fits one amino acid. Only when both are in place does it join the bead to the hook, paying an ATP, from V.5's coupling. There are twenty charging enzymes, one per bead. Each reads a tRNA's shape and picks its bead. They are the true translators; the code lives in their pockets.

Turning a codon message into a bead chain is translation, because it crosses from one alphabet to another, four letters to twenty beads.

The ribosome, tomorrow, does not know the code either. It only checks that a tRNA's anticodon pairs with the codon in front of it. Whatever bead is on the hook goes on the chain. The translators charge; the ribosome trusts.

**A tRNA is a folded RNA with an anticodon at one end and a hook for one bead at the other. Twenty charging enzymes each match one tRNA shape to one bead, paying a coin. They are the true translators.**
