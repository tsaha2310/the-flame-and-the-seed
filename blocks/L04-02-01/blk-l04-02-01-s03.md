A molecule of carbon and hydrogen only is a hydrocarbon. When every bond is single and the carbons form a chain, it is an alkane.

The first eight alkanes, by carbon count: methane, ethane, propane, butane, pentane, hexane, heptane, octane. The first four are old names. From five on, the name counts: pent, hex, hept, oct.

- Methane, one carbon: the gas from a marsh, and from cows.
- Propane and butane, three and four: the cylinder.
- Octane, eight: petrol is mostly chains around this length.
- Twelve to fifteen: kerosene.
- Twenty to thirty: candle wax, solid at room temperature.

Longer chain, higher boiling point. Book II said why: an alkane has only dispersion forces, and dispersion grows with the size of the molecule. Methane is a gas at minus 160. Wax is a solid.

**A hydrocarbon is carbon and hydrogen only. An alkane is one with all single bonds. Longer chains stick together more, so they boil higher.**
