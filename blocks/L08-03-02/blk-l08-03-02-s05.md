Kabir turns crossing over off and his chromosomes pass down whole, good genes chained to bad. He turns it on and the chains break.

**Predict first:** how does a child's chromosome compare to its grandparents'?

<!-- STEP id="run" -->
**Turn the dial.** Without crossing over, each chromosome a child receives is a whole grandparent's chromosome, unchanged. Genes that sit on one chromosome always travel together, for ever: the bundled peas from VIII.2 L03's what-if. A useful spelling on a chromosome next to a harmful one can never escape it.

<!-- STEP id="nails" -->
**What this nails:** Crossing over lets genes on the same chromosome be separated and recombined. It is why a good spelling is not chained to its neighbours. Its rate along a chromosome is also how the first gene maps were drawn.
