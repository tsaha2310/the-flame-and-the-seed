Neither side ever wins. As soon as the parasite's key fits most hosts, the rare hosts with a changed lock have the advantage, and the population shifts to them. As soon as most hosts have the new lock, the rare parasites with a matching key have the advantage. The fit goes up and down, for ever. Each side is changing constantly and neither gets ahead. Two species each evolving in response to the other's changes, with no finish line, is an arms race. The Red Queen in Through the Looking-Glass told Alice she must run as fast as she could to stay in the same place, and biologists use her name for it.

A host that cannot mutate is caught: the parasite's keys keep improving and the host's locks stay the same. A host population of clones is caught almost as fast, because every host has the same lock, and one key opens all of them: the clone field from VIII.1 L04. Hosts that shuffle their genes every generation, from VIII.3, present new combinations of locks each time, and the parasite is always a step behind. The arms race is the best-supported answer to why sex exists at all: shuffling keeps parasites guessing.

The same race runs between plants and the caterpillars that eat them, from VII.4; between antibiotics and bacteria, from VIII.5 L03; between the malaria parasite and every human defence, including sickle haemoglobin.

**An arms race is two species each evolving in response to the other, with no end. A host that stops changing loses, and hosts that shuffle their genes every generation are hardest to catch, which is the best reason we have for sex.**
