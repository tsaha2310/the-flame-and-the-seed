Without decomposers, dead leaves and dead snails pile up, and the carbon and nitrogen in them are locked away. The soil's usable nitrogen falls as the plants take it up and nothing gives it back. By the second month the plants yellow, from VIII.7 L01, and the jar runs down with its nutrients stacked in corpses.

Add the fungi and bacteria that eat dead things. They take a dead leaf apart with the same cutters and acid bags as V.3, burn what they can on the staircase, and release the rest: carbon as carbon dioxide back to the air, nitrogen as ammonia and nitrate back to the soil. A living thing that feeds on the dead and returns their atoms to soil and air is a decomposer. Most are fungi and bacteria; earthworms and beetles help. They are the arrow in the web that runs from everything back to the producers.

Nitrogen goes round its own loop. From the air, fixed by rhizobia and by lightning, into plants as protein, into animals, into the dead, back to soil by decomposers, into plants again, and, by other bacteria, some of it back to the air as gas. That loop is the nitrogen cycle. Water goes round too: up from leaves by evaporation, from II.4, down as condensation on the glass. Energy flows through once, from VIII.8 L02. Atoms go round for ever.

**Decomposers take the dead apart and return carbon to the air and nitrogen to the soil. Without them, atoms pile up in corpses and the producers starve. Energy flows through; atoms cycle; the decomposers close every loop.**
