Water: 1 + 1 + 16 = 18. One mole of water weighs 18 g.

Carbon dioxide: 12 + 16 + 16 = 44. One mole weighs 44 g.

The seed's sugar: 6 times 12, plus 12 times 1, plus 6 times 16, is 72 + 12 + 96 = 180. One mole of it weighs 180 g.

The weight of one mole of anything is its molar mass. Add up the relative atomic masses of the atoms in one molecule, and put "grams per mole" after it.

Now the equation is a recipe. 2 H2 + O2 gives 2 H2O means: 4 g of hydrogen and 32 g of oxygen make 36 g of water. Check: 4 + 32 = 36. The balance agrees, as it must.

**Molar mass turns an equation into a recipe in grams. Count the moles, multiply by the molar mass, and the balance can weigh it.**

Nine grams of water is half a mole. A litre of water, 1000 g, is about 55 moles.
