"In 1869," says Ajji, "a Russian chemist called Dmitri Mendeleev was writing a textbook and could not decide what order to put the elements in."

"Alphabetical," says Kabir.

"He had sixty-three of them on cards. Each card had the element's mass and what it did: how it burned, what it dissolved in, what it joined with. He played patience with them on long train journeys."

She puts a deck of cards on the table. Same idea. Sixty-three cards.
