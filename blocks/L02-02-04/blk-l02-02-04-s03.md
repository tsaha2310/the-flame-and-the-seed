Carbon has four outer electrons and makes four shared pairs, one in each direction of a tetrahedron. The angle between any two bonds is the bond angle, and for carbon with four partners it is about 109 degrees.

Join carbons to carbons and every carbon keeps its four directions. A chain of five carbons is not a straight line. It zigzags, because each carbon's two neighbours sit 109 degrees apart, not 180.

Carbon can do this without limit. Carbon-to-carbon bonds are strong, and each carbon still has spare directions for hydrogens or for more carbons, or for branches, or for closing into a ring. No other element does this so well. Book IV is built on it.

**Carbon has four bonds at four corners, and a carbon chain zigzags because each corner is 109 degrees from the next.**
