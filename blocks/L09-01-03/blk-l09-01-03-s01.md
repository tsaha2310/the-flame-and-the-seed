Ajji unfolds the torn page. The handwriting is a child's, careful and round.

"Living things eat, breathe, grow, move, respond, reproduce, and die."

Seven signs. The list from the first night. Under it, in the same hand, a tick against each, and then, in a different pen, one word: FLAME. And below that, pressed hard: "A flame does all of these. So this is wrong."

"I was twelve," says Ajji. "My grandmother had given me the same seed, and the same lens, and the same question. I wrote the list, and then I tested it on the diya, and it passed, and I tore the page out because I was angry. I did not find the answer that monsoon, or the next. I found it in a laboratory, thirty years later, and it is the loop you built yesterday."

"Then why keep the page?"

"Because it is the first step, and everyone takes it, and it is not wrong. It is just a list of what the loop does."
