Live smooth: the mouse dies. Live rough: it lives. Heat-killed smooth: it lives; the dead cannot infect.

The mixture: the mouse dies. Its blood grows smooth, coated, live bacteria, and they stay smooth in every generation after. Rough bacteria have become smooth, permanently. Something passed from the heat-killed smooth cells into the living rough ones and gave them the coat and the deadliness, and their descendants inherited it.

Griffith called it the transforming principle, and the change transformation. He did not know what the something was. It was a molecule, because the dead cells were dead: no enzymes worked, from V.4's boiled-seed dial. And it was tough enough to survive the heat that unfolded every protein in the cell.

You know one molecule that survives boiling and carries information. Griffith did not. He died in 1941, in a bombing raid, still not knowing.

**A dead cell's instructions can pass into a living cell and change it, and the change is inherited. The carrier is a molecule that survives heat, and in 1928 nobody knew which.**
