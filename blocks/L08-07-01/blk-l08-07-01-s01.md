The Book VII plant's roots come out of the soil with pink lumps on them, the size of moong seeds.

"Inside each one," says Ajji, "are bacteria. The pink is a protein like your blood's, keeping oxygen away from an enzyme that would be poisoned by it. The enzyme breaks the air's nitrogen apart, which no plant and no animal can do. Book II said why: a triple bond. The plant pays the bacteria in sugar. Draw up the deal."
