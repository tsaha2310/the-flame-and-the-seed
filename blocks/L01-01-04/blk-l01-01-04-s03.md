Water is always made from 1 gram of hydrogen for every 8 grams of oxygen. Put in 2 and 8, and one gram of hydrogen is left over. Put in 1 and 10, and 2 grams of oxygen sit there unused. There is no water that is 1 to 7, or 1 to 9.

Every compound has a recipe like this. Salt, sugar, rust. Fixed, by mass. This is the law of definite proportions.

In 1803 John Dalton asked why. His answer: every element is made of tiny identical grains that cannot be split. He called them atoms. If water is always two hydrogen grains stuck to one oxygen grain, then the recipe is fixed, because you cannot use half a grain.

Two thousand years earlier in India, the thinker Kanada had said that all matter is made of unbreakable particles he called anu. He had no balance and no experiment. It was a thought. A good thought, but Dalton's atoms came with numbers that could be checked.

**An idea becomes evidence only when a measurement could have proved it wrong, and did not.**

Dalton's atoms explain both laws at once. Conservation of mass: burning only rearranges grains, so the total never changes. Definite proportions: grains join in whole numbers, so recipes are fixed.
