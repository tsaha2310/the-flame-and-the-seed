Stick alone: a clump. Bigger than a cell, harder to eat, but every cell is doing everything, and the inner cells are starved by the surface rule from V.1 L04. It cannot respond to anything as a whole.

Stick and signal: a clump whose cells warn each other. When one edge dries, the far side knows within seconds, from VII.1 and VII.4. Still every cell does everything.

Stick, signal, and different switches: the cells at the surface open the pages for a tough coat, from VI.6. The inner cells open pages for storage. The cells at the base open pages for gripping the soil. Each cell does one thing well, and the signals coordinate them. The clump has become a body: it can survive a dry day, resist a caterpillar, and grow in a shape.

A living thing made of many cells that stick, signal and take on different jobs is multicellular. It evolved many separate times: plants, animals, fungi and several kinds of seaweed each did it on their own. All three abilities were needed each time, and each was borrowed from something a single cell already did.

**A body is cells that stick, signal, and flip different switches. Stick alone is a clump. All three together is a plant, a fungus, or you.**
