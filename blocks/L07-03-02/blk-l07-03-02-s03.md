At low concentration, a signal molecule rarely finds a switch pocket and, when it does, soon leaves. Few switches are bent. The genes stay off. As the concentration climbs, more switches are bent at any moment. The glow genes' copying rate climbs with them, and the glow appears.

The flip is sharp, not gradual, because the switch works in pairs and the glow genes also make more of the signal, from V.4's feedback run the other way: a little glow makes more signal, which makes more glow. Below a certain concentration the loop cannot get going; above it, it runs to full. That concentration is the threshold. The same word as the nerve's, from VII.2, for the same reason: a small push does nothing and a push past the line does everything.

What sets the threshold is the pocket. A tighter fit means fewer molecules are needed to keep enough switches bent, and the threshold falls. A looser fit raises it. A single change in the switch protein's sequence, from VI.4, moves the threshold, and so changes how many cells must gather before the colony acts.

**A threshold is the concentration at which a switch flips from mostly off to mostly on. The switch's pocket sets it, and self-reinforcing feedback makes the flip sharp.**
