The nucleus is wrapped in two bilayers, from V.1: the nuclear envelope. Pierced through both, at thousands of points, are ring-shaped gates of protein: nuclear pores. Water, ions and small proteins diffuse through freely. Large proteins are stopped, unless they carry a short bead sequence that a carrier protein grips, from Lock and Shape, and escorts them through. Copyists and spool proteins carry such a tag and are let in. Finished mRNA is escorted out. DNA is never let out at all.

Inside, the DNA on its spools, from VI.1 L03, is chromatin: "coloured stuff", from the dyes that first showed it. It is not one thing. Some regions are loosely wound, the spools spread out, and a copyist can land on a promoter there. Other regions are packed tight, spool on spool, and no copyist can reach them. Which regions are loose and which tight differs from cell to cell, and that difference is VI.6.

**The nucleus has a double wall pierced by pores that let small things through and escort tagged large things. Inside, the DNA on its spools, chromatin, is loose where it can be read and tight where it cannot.**
