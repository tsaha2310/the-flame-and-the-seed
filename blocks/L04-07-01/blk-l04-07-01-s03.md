The phosphate of one nucleotide condenses onto the sugar of the next, at the hydroxyl on the sugar's third carbon. Water leaves. Repeat, and the sugars and phosphates alternate down the chain like a spine: sugar, phosphate, sugar, phosphate. That spine is the sugar-phosphate backbone. It is the same in every nucleotide, so it carries no information; it only holds the chain together.

The bases stick out from the backbone, one per sugar, like teeth on a comb. They vary. A, G, C, U, in any order. The order is the information.

The chain has a direction. One end has a free phosphate on the sugar's fifth carbon; chemists call it the five-prime end. The other end has a free hydroxyl on a sugar's third carbon; the three-prime end. Sequences are read from five-prime to three-prime, the way a protein is read from its amine end.

A chain of nucleotides built on this sugar, ribose, is RNA: ribonucleic acid. The word acid is from the phosphates, which give up hydrogen ions.

**RNA is nucleotides chained by condensation into a sugar-phosphate backbone, with the bases hanging off it in order. The backbone is the same everywhere; the order of bases is the information.**
