Both hormones come from small clusters of cells in the pancreas, the gland behind the stomach, and each cluster senses blood glucose directly: glucose enters the cells through carriers and its level sets how much hormone they release.

When glucose is high, after a meal, one cluster releases insulin. Insulin fits receptors on muscle and fat cells and, through a relay, sends more glucose carriers to their walls, from V.2, so they take glucose in. It tells the liver to deposit, from V.5 L02. The line falls.

When glucose is low, between meals, the other cluster releases glucagon. Glucagon fits liver receptors and, through cyclic AMP, the same relay as adrenaline, makes the liver withdraw, from V.5 L03. The line rises.

Two hormones, opposite jobs, each released in proportion to how far the line is from the middle. It is the feedback factory from V.4 and the buffer from III.7, done with cells and blood instead of one enzyme. A thermostat with a heater and a cooler.

**Insulin, when sugar is high, makes cells take glucose in and the liver store it. Glucagon, when sugar is low, makes the liver release it. Two opposite hormones, each proportional, hold the line.**
