The plant's tip is a meristem, from VII.5 L02, and it makes a hormone, from VII.1, that flows down the stem and keeps the side buds dormant. Pinch out the tip and the hormone stops. Within days the side buds wake, each one a meristem of its own, and the plant branches. Gardeners have used this for thousands of years to make bushes out of stalks. The buds were always able; a fading signal held them.

An animal has no tips and does not grow from its ends. Instead, scattered through many tissues, are cells that stay uncommitted, with most of their pages closed but not locked, from VI.6. When a neighbour is lost, signals from the damage wake one, it divides, and its daughters take on the missing job. A cell that stays able to divide and become several kinds is a stem cell. Skin, gut lining, blood and bone each keep their own.

Meristems and stem cells are the same idea in two arrangements: a reserve of cells whose switches are not yet set, held in check by signals until they are needed. A plant keeps its reserve at its tips and can regrow a whole plant from a cutting. An animal keeps its reserve scattered and can mend a wound but not, usually, regrow a limb.

**A plant's tip keeps its side buds asleep with a hormone; prune it and they wake. An animal keeps uncommitted stem cells scattered through its tissues, woken by damage. Both are reserves of cells with their switches unset.**
