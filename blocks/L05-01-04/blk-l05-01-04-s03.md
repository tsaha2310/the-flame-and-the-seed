The cell's food and oxygen come in through its skin. How much can come in depends on how much skin there is: the surface area. How much is needed depends on how much cell there is to feed: the volume.

Double the radius, and the surface area goes up four times. The volume goes up eight times. Every doubling, the cell has twice as much inside to feed per bit of skin. At ten times the radius, it has ten times less skin per bit of volume. And the walk from skin to centre, from III.5, takes a hundred times longer.

So a large cell starves at the centre and suffocates there, even with plenty outside. Below about a tenth of a millimetre, diffusion keeps up. Above that, it cannot.

There are two escapes. Flatten the cell, or fold its surface, so that no part of the inside is far from skin. The mitochondrion's inner folds from yesterday are exactly this. The other escape is to stay small and be many, which is Book VII.

**A cell is fed through its surface and burns through its volume. Surface grows with the square of size, volume with the cube, so a big cell starves at its centre.**
