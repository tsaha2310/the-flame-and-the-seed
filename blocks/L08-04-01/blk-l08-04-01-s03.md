The R factor is a gene for the starch-branching enzyme, from V.5 L02, the one that cuts a stretch off a glucose chain and reattaches it as a fork. The r spelling has an extra stretch of eight hundred letters dropped into the middle of the gene, a mutation from VI.4. The message is read into a chain that folds wrong and does nothing.

RR: two working copies; the seed's starch is branched and sops up water; the seed swells full and dries smooth. rr: no working copy; the starch is long unbranched chains that pack tight and hold less water, so the seed is sweeter, since more sugar stays unconverted, and it shrinks as it dries. Wrinkled. Rr: one working copy makes enough enzyme; the starch is branched; the seed is round.

The different spellings of one gene are alleles. R and r are two alleles of the branching-enzyme gene. What alleles an organism carries is its genotype: RR, Rr or rr. What it shows is its phenotype: round or wrinkled. The Punnett square from VIII.2 counted genotypes; Mendel counted phenotypes.

**An allele is one spelling of a gene. Round is the working spelling of the starch-branching enzyme; wrinkled is a broken one. Genotype is the spellings carried; phenotype is what shows.**

Mendel's factor was a gene, from Book VI. His dominant and recessive were a working enzyme and a broken one, from Book V. Everything he counted was chemistry.
