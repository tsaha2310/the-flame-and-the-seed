Any change to the tape's letters is a mutation. Four kinds, by what they do to the protein.

- Silent: the new codon names the same bead, from VI.3's spares. The chain is unchanged. Most third-letter changes.
- Missense: the new codon names a different bead. One bead swapped. From IV.5 L05, that may do nothing, may break the fold, or may make a sticky patch, depending on where it sits.
- Nonsense: the new codon is a stop. The chain ends early. Usually the protein is useless.
- Frameshift: a letter is deleted or inserted. The ribosome still reads in threes, so every codon after the change is read in the wrong frame. Every bead after that point is wrong, and a stop usually appears within a few codons. Delete three letters, though, and the frame is restored: one bead missing and the rest intact.

**A mutation is a changed letter. Silent changes nothing, missense swaps one bead, nonsense stops the chain, and a frameshift, one letter in or out, scrambles everything after it.**

Where mutations come from, and how rare they are, is Book VIII. For now: they happen, and the code's spares make most of them silent.
