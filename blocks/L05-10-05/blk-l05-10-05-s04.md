Respiration is universal and runs downhill: it uses sugar and oxygen. Only photosynthesis runs the other way, and only in the light, with carbon dioxide to attach. Remove light, or carbon dioxide, or the green cells themselves, and the loop is cut at the top. What is left is a slow fire burning down through its stores.

The oxygen in the air is an old bank balance, built up by billions of years of leaves and their ancestors. It would take a very long time to spend. The food is a daily wage, and stops the day the leaves stop.

The diya's oil, the peanut, the coal in a power station, the petrol in a car: all of them are sugar that a leaf made and something stored, some of it three hundred million years ago. Book VIII returns to that.

**Every carbon in every living thing entered through a leaf, and every oxygen it burns with was set free from water by a leaf. Cut photosynthesis and the world runs down in a year, on stored food, long before the air runs out.**
