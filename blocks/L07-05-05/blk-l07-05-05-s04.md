Every cell in a body is told, constantly, by touch and by molecules from its neighbours, whether to divide, to do its job, or to die. The stop signals are as many as the go signals. A cell whose receptors or relays for a stop signal are broken, by a mutation in a dividing cell, from VI.4, divides anyway. If its daughters inherit the fault they divide too. That is the start of a tumour. Most such cells are caught: the body's defences and the cell's own checks kill most rogues before they matter. Cancer is the rare one that also gains the changes that let it evade the checks and, later, unstick and travel.

A plant without meristems cannot grow or mend. An animal with one tip cannot mend anything but its head. Reserves must be spread to where damage happens, and held in check until it does.

**A cell that cannot hear stop divides into a lump: the beginning of cancer, a cheater inside a body. A body needs reserves of dividing cells wherever it may be damaged, and signals that keep them waiting.**
