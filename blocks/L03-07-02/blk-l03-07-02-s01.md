Ajji has a pH meter from her old lab, a pen with a metal tip. She dips it in nimbu juice. It reads 2.4. In vinegar: 2.8. In tap water: 7.2. In soap water: 9.5.

"Nimbu is 2.4 and vinegar is 2.8," says Kabir. "So nimbu is a little bit more acid."

"Four tenths of a step," says Ajji. "About two and a half times more. And the chemist's acid at 1 is about twenty-five times stronger than nimbu again."
