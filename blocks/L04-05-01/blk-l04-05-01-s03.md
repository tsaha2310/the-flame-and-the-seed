Every bead has the same core: a central carbon holding a hydrogen, a carboxyl group from IV.3, and a new group, a nitrogen with two hydrogens on it. A nitrogen with hydrogens is an amine group, and it is the opposite of a carboxyl: it takes a hydrogen ion instead of giving one. So each bead has a sour end and a base end. A molecule with an amine and a carboxyl is an amino acid.

The fourth thing on the central carbon is different on every bead: the side chain. Twenty side chains, twenty amino acids.

The tests sort the twenty into four personalities.

- Oily: side chains that are hydrocarbon, squeezed out of water. About eight of the twenty.
- Polar: side chains with a hydroxyl or an amide, happy in water, no charge. About seven.
- Plus: side chains that carry an extra amine, which takes a hydrogen ion and becomes positive. Three.
- Minus: side chains with an extra carboxyl, which gives a hydrogen ion and becomes negative. Two.

Two of the twenty carry sulphur. One of those can hold hands with another of its kind across a molecule, and that will matter in two lessons.

**An amino acid is a core with a sour end, a base end, and one of twenty side chains. The side chain gives each one a personality: oily, polar, plus or minus.**

The white in milk is chains of these beads. The nimbu's acid changed the charges on the plus and minus beads, and chains that had been repelling each other clumped. That is paneer.
