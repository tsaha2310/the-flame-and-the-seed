The repressor handles one question: is lactose here? The helper handles the other: is glucose scarce?

When glucose is scarce, the cell's level of a small messenger molecule rises. It fits the helper's second pocket, bends it, from allostery, and the bent helper grips the DNA just before the promoter and makes the copyist land far more easily. When glucose is plentiful, the messenger falls, the helper lets go, and the copyist barely lands even with the repressor gone.

Put the two together. The gene runs hard only when lactose is present AND glucose is NOT. Lactose present, glucose present: repressor off, helper off, a trickle. Lactose absent: repressor on, nothing, whatever the glucose. Lactose present, glucose gone: repressor off, helper on, full speed. The bacterium eats glucose first, then switches to lactose, with a pause in between while the cutters are made. Jacob and Monod saw that pause in growth curves before they knew what caused it.

**Two proteins, each bent by one signal, wire a gene to run only when lactose is present and glucose is not. A gene's switch can compute an AND and a NOT.**

Nobody computes. Two shaped proteins, two small molecules bumping into them, and the copying rate follows. The logic is in the shapes.
