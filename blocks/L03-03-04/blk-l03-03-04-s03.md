The thing that takes electrons is an oxidant, because taking electrons from something oxidises it. Oxygen, chlorine, copper ions. The strongest oxidants sit top right of the table, where the pull is greatest. Oxygen is not the only one, but it is the one the air is full of.

The thing that gives electrons is a reductant, because giving electrons to something reduces it. Sodium, iron, zinc, hydrogen, the carbon in fuels. The strongest reductants sit bottom left of the table, where the outer electron is barely held.

The metal ladder from III.1 is now a list of reductants in order of generosity. Magnesium gives most easily. Zinc next. Iron. Copper last. A metal higher up will give its electrons to the ions of any metal lower down. That is displacement, and it is now a redox with a reason.

**An oxidant takes electrons. A reductant gives them. Every redox has one of each, and the Tug-of-War says which is which.**

The apple. Cut it, and molecules inside meet oxygen from the air. Something in the apple, a helper you will meet in Book V, speeds their oxidation to brown pigments. Nimbu juice has a molecule that is a better reductant than the apple's: vitamin C. It gives its electrons to the oxygen first. The apple stays pale until the vitamin C is used up.
