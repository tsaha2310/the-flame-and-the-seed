Two chlorophyll towers in the thylakoid membrane, each with hundreds of chlorophylls funnelling photons to one special pair at the centre.

First lift. A photon lifts an electron from the first tower's special pair up a rung. From there the electron falls down a short staircase of pullers, from V.7, to the second tower. As it falls, ions are pumped across the thylakoid membrane, into the disc. A dam is filling, from V.7 L04.

Second lift. A photon lifts the same electron from the second tower, higher than any single lift could. From there it falls a short way onto a carrier: NADP+, a cousin of NAD+ with one extra phosphate. Full, it is NADPH. It holds electrons high enough to push carbon dioxide into sugar. That is what it is for.

The hole. The first tower has lost an electron, and it is now the strongest puller in the leaf, stronger than oxygen. It tears an electron from water. Two water molecules, four electrons, four hydrogen ions into the disc, and one oxygen molecule bubbles off. That is the oxygen the plant gives away: water torn apart, by a hole a photon made. Book I split water with a battery; the leaf splits it with light.

These steps, photons in, electrons lifted twice, water split, NADPH filled, dam filled, are the light reactions.

**Two lifts: a photon raises an electron, it falls down a short staircase pumping ions, a second photon raises it higher, and it lands on NADPH. The hole is filled from water, and oxygen is set free.**
