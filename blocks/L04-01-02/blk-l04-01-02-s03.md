Every carbon has four bonds pointing at four corners. Join carbons to carbons and they keep going. A line of them is a chain. A chain with a carbon sticking out sideways is a branched chain. A chain joined end to end is a ring. Carbon can also share two pairs with a neighbour, a double bond, or three, a triple bond.

The carbon framework of a molecule, with the hydrogens ignored, is its carbon skeleton. Everything on the ladder from here is a skeleton with things hung on it.

Two facts make carbon the builder. First, four directions in three dimensions, from Book II: a carbon can hold a chain going on, a branch, and two more things at once. Second, the carbon-to-carbon bond is strong, about as strong as carbon-to-hydrogen and carbon-to-oxygen. A carbon chain does not have a weak link. It is as strong in the middle as at the ends, so it can be as long as you like.

**Carbon builds because it bonds in four directions and its bonds to itself are as strong as its bonds to anything else. Chains, branches and rings follow.**

There is no upper limit. A grain of rice holds chains thousands of carbons long, with sugar rings hung along them.
