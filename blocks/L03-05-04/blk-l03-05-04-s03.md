Water molecules random-walk through the holes in both directions. Salt ions cannot; they are too big with their water shells on.

On the salty side, some of the space near the wall is taken up by salt. Fewer water molecules bump into the holes from that side. On the pure side, every bump is water. So more water crosses toward the salty side than back. The salty side's level rises.

This is osmosis: water moving through a wall that lets water through but not what is dissolved in it, from where water is more crowded to where it is less. A wall that chooses like this is semi-permeable, which means "partly passable".

The cucumber's insides are watery and only a little salty. The bath is very salty. Water leaves the cucumber. The raisin's insides are sugary; the bath is pure water. Water enters. The grape in syrup shrivels like the cucumber.

**Osmosis is diffusion of water through a sieve that stops the solute. Water goes toward the side with more dissolved stuff.**

Nobody pumps. It is the random walk from yesterday, with a wall that only some can pass.
