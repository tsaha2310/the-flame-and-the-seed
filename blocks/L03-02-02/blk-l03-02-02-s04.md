The chemist's dozen is 602,200,000,000,000,000,000,000. Six hundred and two thousand billion billion. Written short, 6.022 times ten to the twenty-third. It is called Avogadro's number, after the man whose idea led to it.

That many hydrogen atoms weigh one gram. That many carbon atoms weigh twelve grams. That many oxygen atoms weigh sixteen. The relative atomic mass, with "grams" after it, is the weight of one dozen.

The dozen has a name: a mole. One mole of anything is Avogadro's number of that thing.

At a million a second, one mole takes about nineteen billion years to count. The universe is about fourteen billion years old. Every person on Earth at a billion a second still needs over two years.

**A mole is a count, like a dozen, big enough that a mole of hydrogen atoms weighs one gram. It is the bridge between atoms and the balance.**

A spoon of water is about a mole of molecules. You have never held anything that was not a very large number of moles or a very small fraction of one.
