The tagged particle does not travel in a line. It bumps into a neighbour, bounces, bumps another, bounces. Each bounce sends it in a new direction that has nothing to do with the last. This is a random walk.

A random walker gets somewhere, but slowly. Doubling the distance takes four times as many steps, not two. That is why the ink takes ten minutes to fill a jug but a smell crosses a verandah in under a minute: air is thin, so each step is long.

A thousand walkers released at one side spread out until both sides have the same number. Not because any walker is aiming for the empty side. Because there are more walkers on the crowded side to wander away from it than there are on the empty side to wander back. The spreading of particles from where they are crowded to where they are not is diffusion.

Hotter means faster steps, so faster diffusion. Bigger particles get knocked less far per bump, so they diffuse slower. Sugar diffuses through a glass in an hour; an ion, in minutes; a smell through air, in seconds.

**Every particle takes a random walk. Diffusion is what the walks add up to: crowded to empty, with nobody aiming.**
