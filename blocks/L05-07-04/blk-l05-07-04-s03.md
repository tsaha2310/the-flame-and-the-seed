The chain pumped hydrogen ions into the gap between the walls. Now the gap is crowded with them and the matrix is not, and because each ion is a charge, the gap is positive and the matrix negative. A difference in ion crowding and in charge, across a wall, is a proton gradient. Proton is the other name for a hydrogen ion, from I.3. It is a battery, from V.2, built not by a pump spending coins but by electrons falling.

Flowing back into the matrix is downhill for the ions on both counts, from I.2's charges and III.5's diffusion. The inner wall stops them, from V.2 L01: ions cannot cross a bilayer. The only way through is a protein that spans the wall with a channel through its base and a rotor on top. Ions flow through the channel, and as they pass, they turn the rotor. The rotor's turning bends the head, and the bending of the head, from Lock and Shape, presses ADP and phosphate together into ATP. Three ions per coin. The protein is ATP synthase, and it truly spins, about a hundred times a second.

That is the second wall's reason. A dam needs two sides. The chain fills one; the turbine drains it into the other; and the coins are made by the flow between them. The energy of the electrons' fall passed through a gradient before it became ATP. It did not go straight from food to coin.

Per glucose: ten NADH and two other carriers empty into the chain, the dam fills and drains, and the turbine makes about twenty-six coins. Add the four from before: about thirty.

**The chain fills a dam of hydrogen ions between the two walls. The ions flow back through ATP synthase, a turbine that spins and presses coins. The fall goes through the gradient, not straight into ATP.**

Close the channel and the dam fills to the brim, and the chain, unable to push against a full dam, stops. Cut the oxygen and the chain has nowhere to send electrons; it stops; the dam drains; the turbine stops. Everything upstream of oxygen waits on it.
