Copper, iron, aluminium, magnesium, sodium, tin: they bend instead of breaking, they shine when scratched, and they light the LED. These are metals. They sit on the left and middle of the table, where the pull on outer electrons is weak.

Sulphur, charcoal, iodine, argon: they crumble or are gases, they are dull, and they do not conduct. Charcoal is the one exception that conducts a little. These are non-metals. They sit on the right, where the pull is strong.

Silicon and boron: shiny but brittle, and they conduct a little, and more when warm. These are metalloids. They sit on the staircase line between the two sides.

Why does a metal bend and shine? Because its outer electrons are held so weakly that they are shared by the whole lump. That idea gets a lesson of its own in Book II.

**Weak pull on outer electrons makes a metal. Strong pull makes a non-metal. The staircase is where the pull is in between.**
