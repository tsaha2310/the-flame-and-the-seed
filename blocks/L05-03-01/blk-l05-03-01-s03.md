The wall cannot let a bacterium through; it is a thousand times too big for any channel. So the cell does something a bilayer is built to do: it flows. The wall grips the bacterium with shaped proteins on its surface, from Lock and Shape, then spreads around it from all sides. Where the edges meet, they fuse, because a bilayer's edges always close, from V.1 L01. The result is a bubble of wall inside the cell, with the bacterium inside the bubble. The cell has taken in a large thing by wrapping it. That is endocytosis: "into the cell".

Notice that the bacterium is still on the outside, in a sense. The bubble's inner face was the wall's outer face a moment ago. The bacterium has never touched the cell's insides. It is in a room the cell built around it.

The white blood cell that does this hunting is a macrophage: "big eater". Your scrape is full of them.

**A cell takes in something too big for any channel by wrapping its wall around it and pinching off a bubble. That is endocytosis, and a macrophage lives by it.**

Every swallow costs wall. Five bacteria, and a noticeable share of the cell's surface is now inside it as bubbles. Something must give it back, which is two lessons away.
