A feature that comes in versions you can see, tall or short, round or wrinkled, purple or white, is a trait. Mendel picked seven that came in exactly two versions each and never blended.

He started with plants that bred true: tall parents that gave only tall children, generation after generation. A line like that is a pure line.

Cross a pure tall with a pure short, and every child is tall. Not medium. Not a mix. The short version has vanished. A child of two different pure lines is a hybrid.

Cross the hybrids with each other, and the short version comes back, in about one grandchild in four. Three tall to one short. Round versus wrinkled: three to one. Yellow versus green: three to one. Purple versus white: three to one. Every trait, the same ratio, and only with large numbers; ten plants give any ratio at all.

Mendel had the counts. What he did next was the physicist's part: he asked what hidden rule would give exactly three to one, every time.

**A trait comes in versions. A pure line breeds true. Cross two pure lines and the hybrids all show one version; cross the hybrids and the hidden version returns in one child in four.**
