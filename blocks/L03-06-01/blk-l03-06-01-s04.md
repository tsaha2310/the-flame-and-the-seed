Red and blue only react when they collide. Not every collision works; only ones hard enough. The number of successful collisions per second is the rate of reaction.

Warmer: particles move faster. They collide more often, and more of the collisions are hard enough. Rate goes up steeply. Ten degrees warmer often doubles the rate. That is the milk: eight hours on the shelf is worth two days in the fridge.

More concentrated: more red in the same space, so blue meets red more often. Rate goes up.

Powder instead of lump: the lump's inside cannot be hit; only its surface can. Powder is nearly all surface. Rate goes up. That is why the powdered sugar dissolves first, and why flour dust in a mill can explode when a sack of flour will not even burn well.

**Reactions happen through collisions. Warmer, more concentrated, and more surface all mean more collisions that count.**
