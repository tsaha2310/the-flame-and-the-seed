Kabir builds his alien. It falls apart in the bench's air and what is left burns to a small heap of sand.

"It would breathe out sand," he says. "It would fill up with sand from the inside."

**Predict first:** can a silicon chain be as long as a carbon chain, and what does a silicon creature breathe out?

<!-- STEP id="run" -->
**Turn the dial.** The silicon-silicon bond is about half as strong as carbon-carbon, and the silicon-oxygen bond is far stronger than either. A silicon chain longer than a few atoms breaks up in air, because every silicon would rather be bonded to oxygen. Silicon's methane catches fire on contact with air. And the burn test: carbon burns to carbon dioxide, a gas that floats away. Silicon burns to silicon dioxide, which is sand. Solid at room temperature, solid at a thousand degrees.

<!-- STEP id="nails" -->
**What this nails:** Carbon's chains are strong and its oxide is a gas. Silicon's chains are weak and its oxide is a rock. A silicon creature could not hold together in air, and could not breathe out sand.
