Coupling, from III.8, wastes whatever part of the fall the lift does not use. Couple a huge fall to a tiny lift, and almost all of it is wasted, as heat.

Glucose burning is a huge fall: enough for about thirty ATP coins. Most jobs in the seed are tiny lifts: joining two beads, pushing one ion across a wall, bending one protein. Pay for a tiny lift with the whole glucose fall and you waste twenty-nine thirtieths.

ATP's fall is the right size for one job. So the seed does two things. It breaks the glucose fall into about thirty small falls, and it uses each small fall to recharge one ATP. Then it spends the ATPs, one per job. Little is wasted at each step, and the shop stays cool.

**A coin must match the size of the jobs. ATP is small change. Glucose is a big note that must be broken before it can be spent without burning the shop down.**

The flame does not break the note. It spends the whole fall at once, as heat and light. That is the short circuit from III.9, said in coins.
