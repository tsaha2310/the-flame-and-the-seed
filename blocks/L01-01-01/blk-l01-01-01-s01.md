Ajji has taken the kitchen balance down from the shelf, the one she weighs flour on.

She puts a ball of steel wool on it. The scrubbing kind, from under the sink. The display says 5.0 g.

"Kabir says the flame eats," she says. "Let us see what eating does to the food."

She touches a match to the wool. It does not burst into flame. It glows orange and crackles, and the glow crawls through it like a slow spark. Kabir's mouth is open.

"Watch the number," says Ajji.
