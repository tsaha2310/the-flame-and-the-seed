The cutting's genome matches the parent's in all but a few letters out of hundreds of millions: the slips that got through proofreading during the divisions that built it, from VIII.1 L02. A new living thing made by mitosis alone, so that it carries a copy of its parent's tape, is a clone. A cutting is one. A yeast bud is one. Each half of a cut hydra is one. Every cell in your body is a clone of the egg you began as, with its own few slips.

Cloning is fast and needs no partner. A plant that is well suited to its spot can fill it with copies of itself. Gardeners and farmers use it constantly: every banana you have eaten is a cutting of a cutting of one plant, and so is every alphonso mango.

And it has a cost that is the rest of this Book. Every clone is nearly the same. If a disease comes for one, it comes for all. A field of clones is a field with one set of locks, waiting for one key. The few slips per copy are the only source of difference, and they are slow.

**A clone is a living thing made by mitosis alone: a cutting, a bud, a healed half. It carries its parent's tape with only a handful of slips. Fast, no partner, and nearly all the same.**
