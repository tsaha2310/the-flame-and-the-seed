"Gregor Mendel," says Ajji, "was a monk in Brno, in what is now the Czech Republic, and a trained physicist who had failed his teaching exams. In 1856 he started crossing pea plants in the monastery garden and counting their children. Eight years. About twenty-eight thousand plants. He found rules that nobody believed for thirty-five years."

"Rules of what?"

"Of how a seed gets its traits from two parents instead of one. Find them yourself. The garden has the rules hidden in it."
