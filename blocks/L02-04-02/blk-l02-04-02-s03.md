Methane: minus 162 degrees. Dispersion forces only, and a small molecule. The weakest stickiness on the table. A gas everywhere on Earth.

Water: 100 degrees. Hydrogen bonds, four per molecule. For such a small molecule, a very high boiling point. Straight water would be down near methane.

Salt: about 1400 degrees. To boil it you must pull ions apart from their whole checkerboard, and every ion is held by six neighbours.

Iron: about 2900 degrees. The metallic bond, ions in a sea, is very strong, and every ion is held by the whole lump.

Diamond: about 3500 degrees before it comes apart. There are no molecules to separate; the whole crystal is one giant covalent web. Boiling it means breaking shared pairs, the strongest grip of all.

**Boiling point follows the strength of what holds the particles together. Weak pulls between small molecules boil first; giant lattices of bonds boil last.**

Notice the trap. "Share" is the grip inside water and inside diamond. Water boils at 100 and diamond at 3500. The difference is that water is small molecules held to each other by weak pulls, and diamond is one molecule the size of the crystal.
