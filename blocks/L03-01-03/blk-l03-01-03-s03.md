Four families cover everything on the board.

Two or more things join into one: a combination. Magnesium and oxygen give magnesium oxide. Hydrogen and oxygen give water.

One thing splits into two or more: a decomposition. Heat chuna-stone and it gives chuna and carbon dioxide. Pass a current through water and it gives hydrogen and oxygen. Decompositions usually need a push: heat, light, or electricity, because they run uphill.

One element pushes another out of a compound and takes its place: a displacement. Iron pushes copper out of copper sulphate. Zinc pushes hydrogen out of acid. The one that does the pushing is the one that pulls electrons less hard, and gives them up more easily. That is the Tug-of-War again, with a preview of Book III's electricity module.

A fuel joins oxygen fast, with heat and light: a combustion. Methane, wax, the diya's oil, and, slowed to a crawl and hidden, the seed.

**Join, split, swap, burn. Every re-gripping on the shelf belongs to one of these four families.**
