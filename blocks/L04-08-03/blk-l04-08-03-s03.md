In every species, the amount of A equals the amount of T. The amount of G equals the amount of C. Human: 30, 30, 20, 20. Cow: 29, 29, 21, 21. Bacterium: 25 of each.

But the split between the pairs differs. Some species are A-T rich; some are G-C rich. This is Chargaff's rule: A equals T, G equals C, and the ratio of the two pairs varies.

Chargaff had no idea why. Watson and Crick did, in 1953, when they put the pairs from IV.7 inside Franklin's double helix. Every A on one strand sits across from a T on the other. Every G sits across from a C. So the counts must match. The rule was a fingerprint of pairing.

Two strands where each letter faces its partner are complementary. Give me one strand and I can write the other: swap A for T, G for C, and reverse the direction. Two strands, one message, written twice.

**Chargaff's rule: A equals T, G equals C, in every species. It is true because the two strands of the helix are complementary: every base faces its pair.**
