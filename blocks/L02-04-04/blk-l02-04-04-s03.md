In liquid water, hydrogen bonds form and break constantly. Molecules tumble past each other, and on average they pack fairly close.

At freezing, the tumbling stops. Every molecule locks into place with exactly four hydrogen bonds, two from its hydrogens and two to its lone pairs, in the four directions you found in II.2. That makes a lattice of hexagonal rings, and the rings have holes in the middle.

So ice has fewer molecules in the same box than liquid water does. The amount of stuff in a given space is called density. Ice is less dense than water. Less dense floats.

Nearly every other substance packs tighter as a solid, so its solid sinks. Water is the exception because its molecules lock into an open cage.

**Ice floats because water's hydrogen bonds lock it into an open lattice with holes in it. Same molecules, more space.**

Now the fish. A pond in winter cools from the top. The cold water at the surface freezes into a lid of ice. The lid floats and insulates the water below, which stays liquid at around 4 degrees. The fish live in it until spring.
