Kabir's dominant sickle allele is gone in ten generations. The recessive one lasts a thousand.

**Predict first:** what happens to its frequency, with malaria present?

<!-- STEP id="run" -->
**Turn the dial.** Every carrier is now ill, and the protection against malaria is outweighed by the disease in every person who has the allele. The allele is exposed to selection in every generation and falls to near zero within a few, malaria or not.

<!-- STEP id="nails" -->
**What this nails:** A harmful dominant allele is exposed at once. A harmful recessive hides in carriers, and if the carrier state is useful, it can be common. Whether an allele shows in one copy decides how selection sees it.
