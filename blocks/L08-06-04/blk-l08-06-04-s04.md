The virus card from the Prologue. A virus is a protein coat around a tape, from VI.1 L02. Outside a cell it is a crystal: no wall of its own, no metabolism, no coins, no ribosome. It cannot copy itself. Inside a cell, its tape is read by the cell's machines and the cell builds new viruses. It reproduces, by borrowing everything.

On the tree, it has no branch of its own. Viruses are not one family; different viruses seem to have arisen many times, some from escaped pieces of cells' own tapes, some perhaps from very early copiers. Their tapes are RNA or DNA, in one or two strands. They are filed by their own scheme, off the tree, as things that need a cell.

Is a virus alive? It carries a tape and evolves, from VIII.5, faster than anything. It has no wall, no slow fire, no machines of its own. The Ledger has lines it passes and lines it fails. Hold that until the Epilogue, where it is one of the four cards on the table.

**Filing by habitat mispredicts everything the habitat did not shape. Filing by descent predicts what has not been checked. A virus has no branch: a tape in a coat that borrows a cell's machines, filed off the tree, and an open question.**
