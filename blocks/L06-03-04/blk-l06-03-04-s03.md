Kabir watches the strict code's cell fill with broken machines.

"More kinds of bead, and it is worse."

"More kinds of bead and no slack. The slack is the point."

**Predict first:** same typos; how many more proteins break?

<!-- STEP id="run" -->
**Turn the dial.** Under the real code, about a quarter of typos land on a third letter and change nothing; many others swap a bead for a similar one, because codons for similar beads tend to be neighbours in the table. Most proteins survive. Under the no-spares code, every typo changes a bead, and with sixty-one beads the change is usually to something unlike, so the fold breaks far more often. Three to four times as many broken proteins from the same typos. A cell under the strict code, at the real copying error rate, cannot keep its machines working.

<!-- STEP id="nails" -->
**What this nails:** Redundancy buffers error. The spare words are why a tape can be copied with occasional slips and still make working proteins, and that is what lets it be copied at all. Book VIII depends on it.
