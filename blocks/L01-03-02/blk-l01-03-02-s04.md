With the pudding model, every particle passes through with a small wobble. Nothing comes back. A soft spread of positive charge cannot turn a fast particle around.

With the nuclear model, most go straight through untouched, because most of the atom is empty. But about one in eight thousand hits the tiny centre head-on and is thrown straight back by its charge.

That is what Rutherford's students saw. He said it was like firing a cannon ball at a sheet of paper and having it come back and hit you.

So the atom is mostly empty. At its centre is a very small, very dense, positive core: the nucleus. Around it, far out, are the light negative particles: electrons. The positive charge in the nucleus is carried by particles called protons.

**Almost all of an atom is empty space. Nearly all its mass sits in a tiny positive nucleus, with electrons far outside.**

Remember I.2. Electrons are negative, the nucleus is positive, so the electrons sit low on the Hill near it. That is what holds an atom together.
