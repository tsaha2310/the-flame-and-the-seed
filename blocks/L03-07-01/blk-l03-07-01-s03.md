Three groups. Nimbu, vinegar, dahi, cold tea: turmeric stays yellow, hibiscus goes bright pink, cabbage goes red. Soap, chuna water, baking soda: turmeric goes red, hibiscus goes green, cabbage goes blue-green. Milk and tap water: nothing much.

The cut-away shows the difference. In the first group, some hydrogen atoms have left their molecules and are floating loose in the water as bare hydrogen ions. A hydrogen ion is a proton with no electron: the smallest ion there is. In the second group, there are almost none, and instead some water molecules are missing a hydrogen, floating as hydroxide ions.

An acid is a substance that gives hydrogen ions to water. Nimbu's citric acid, vinegar's acetic acid, dahi's lactic acid. Sour is what your tongue calls a hydrogen ion.

A base is a substance that takes hydrogen ions from water, leaving hydroxide behind. Soap, chuna, baking soda. Slippery is what your fingers call hydroxide: it is turning the oil on your skin into soap.

An indicator is a molecule that changes shape, and so colour, when it gains or loses a hydrogen ion.

**An acid gives hydrogen ions. A base takes them. Sour and slippery are your body's own indicators.**
