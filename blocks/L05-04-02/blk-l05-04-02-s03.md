The starch fragment fits the pocket. As it settles in, the pocket closes around it, from Lock and Shape's second half: a fit that bends the piece. The closing pocket holds the alpha link in a strained position, with charged side chains from IV.5 pressed against exactly the atoms that must move. In that position the link is halfway to broken. The hump for cutting it is far lower than it is in open water. The link breaks. The pieces no longer fit the pocket, and float out. The protein is unchanged and does it again.

The protein is an enzyme. The molecule that fits its pocket is its substrate. The pocket is the active site. An enzyme is a catalyst, from III.6, made of protein, with a pocket shaped for one substrate. It lowers the hump for one reaction and leaves every other reaction alone.

The cellulose fragment does not fit. Its beta link points the other way, and the pocket cannot close on it. No strain, no hump lowered, no cut. That is IV.4's rice: the enzyme fits the alpha link and not the beta, by a few degrees of shape.

**An enzyme is a folded protein with a pocket. The substrate fits the pocket, the pocket closes and strains it, and the hump for one reaction falls. The enzyme bends to fit. It lowers the hump. It never moves the valley.**

The last sentence is III.6's guard rail, and it holds for every enzyme in the seed. An enzyme can make a downhill reaction fast. It cannot make an uphill one run. For that, the cell needs coupling, and the enzyme that does the coupling is the next-but-one lesson.
