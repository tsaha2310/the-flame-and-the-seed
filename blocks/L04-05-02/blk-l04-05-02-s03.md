The amine of one bead meets the carboxyl of the next. One O-H from the carboxyl and one H from the amine leave as water. The carbon and the nitrogen are now joined directly. That link is a peptide bond. It is a condensation, the same move as the ester and the sugar link, on a new rung.

Join beads this way and you get a chain with a free amine at one end and a free carboxyl at the other. It has a direction. Chemists write it from the amine end.

A chain of amino acids that is long enough to fold into a working shape is a protein. Most are between fifty and a few thousand beads. The order of beads along the chain is its sequence, and the sequence is written as a string of letters, one per amino acid.

Two chains of the same ten beads in a different order are two different proteins. The side chains sit in different places, so the chain will fold differently, and that is the whole of the next lesson.

**Amino acids join by peptide bonds into a chain with a direction. The order of beads is the protein's sequence, and there are more possible sequences than atoms in the universe.**

The white in milk is a protein. So is the cutter in your saliva. So is the stuff of muscle, of hair, of the lens in your eye, and of nearly every machine in the seed.
