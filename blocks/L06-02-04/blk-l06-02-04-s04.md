The cell adjusts what it makes by adjusting copying rates, from VI.2 L03, and that only works because copies fade. Make the copies permanent and the cell can only ever add. Every level ratchets up and never down.

That is what the single missing oxygen in DNA's sugar, from IV.8 L02, is for. DNA, with no hydroxyl to cut itself, lasts: right for the master. RNA, with the hydroxyl, cuts itself: right for the message. If the chemist's rule "one oxygen fewer" had gone the other way, the master would rot in a month and the messages would last for years. Neither would work.

**A message that never fades cannot be turned off. The cell controls what it makes through copies that die on their own, and the one-oxygen difference between RNA and DNA is what makes that possible.**
