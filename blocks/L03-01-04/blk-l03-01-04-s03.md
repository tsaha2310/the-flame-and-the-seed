The lies come in two kinds.

The first kind is an equation whose atoms do not match. Fe + O2 gives Fe2O3 looks plausible and is short two irons and one oxygen. Real rust needs 4 Fe + 3 O2 gives 2 Fe2O3. An equation like this claims atoms from nothing. Book I says no.

The second kind is worse, because the pans level. C + O2 gives CO2 is true. C + O2 gives CO + O is balanced by count, but "O" on its own is not a thing that comes out of a fire; oxygen atoms pair up. And H2 + O2 gives H2O2 balances and is a different reaction than the one you wanted. An equation can count right and still describe something that does not happen.

**A balanced equation is necessary. It is not enough. The molecules have to be real and the reaction has to be one that runs.**

Whether a reaction runs, and which way, is the question the rest of this Book answers, on the Hill.
