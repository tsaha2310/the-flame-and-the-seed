The messenger is a signal: a molecule sent by one cell that changes what another cell does. It never enters the liver cell. It fits a receptor, from V.3, on the outside face of the wall, and the fit bends the receptor's fold, from Lock and Shape, so that its inside face changes shape too. The message has crossed the wall without the molecule crossing.

The receptor's new shape activates an enzyme inside. That enzyme makes many copies of a small messenger molecule. Each of those activates another enzyme. Each of those activates many more. The message is passed along a chain of shape changes: a relay.

And at each step, one activates many. One receptor, a hundred enzymes, ten thousand of the next, a million of the next. That growth is amplification. By the end, the last enzymes in the chain switch on glycogen phosphorylase, from V.5 L03, all over the cell, and millions of glucose beads are clipped off the bank and sent into the blood. One molecule in, millions out.

**A signal is a molecule that fits a receptor on a cell's outside. The fit bends the receptor, the bend is relayed inward by a chain of shape changes, and each step activates many of the next: amplification. One molecule in, millions of glucose out.**

The response was the sugar bank paying out. The whole of Book V's machinery, switched by one shape change at the wall.
