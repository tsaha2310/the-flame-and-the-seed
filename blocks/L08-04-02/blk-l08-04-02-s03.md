Dominant means one thing only: the allele whose phenotype shows in a hybrid. Why it shows varies.

Most often, one working copy of an enzyme is enough, as with the round pea. The working allele is dominant; the broken one hides.

Sometimes a broken protein does harm: a receptor that grips the relay and jams it, so a hybrid with one working and one jamming copy still cannot signal. The broken allele is dominant.

Sometimes neither hides. A sickle hybrid, from VI.4, has half normal and half sickle haemoglobin; in ordinary oxygen it is well, and in very low oxygen its blood sickles a little. Both alleles show, in different conditions.

And dominant never means common, or strong, or better. The dominant allele for one form of dwarfism is rare and harmful. The recessive allele for blue eyes is common in some populations. A recipe, not a drawing: dominance is about how two recipes' products behave together in a cell, not about which recipe is favoured.

**Dominant only means it shows in a hybrid. Usually that is because one working copy of an enzyme suffices. Sometimes a broken protein jams a working one and the broken allele is dominant. Never does dominant mean common or better.**
