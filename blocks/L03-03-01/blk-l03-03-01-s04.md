Dry air: almost no rust. Fully under still water: a little. Half in, half out: a lot. Salt water: the most, and fastest.

Rust needs oxygen and water together. The half-wet nail has both at the waterline. Salt water conducts, and rusting, as you will see in III.9, is partly an electrical business, so salt speeds it.

The rust itself is iron joined to oxygen, with some water tangled in. Iron oxide. The same family as the black stuff on the steel wool, which was iron oxide made fast and hot.

The apple's cut surface browns because molecules in the apple join with oxygen from the air. Cover the cut and it stays pale. Squeeze nimbu on it and it stays pale too, for a reason that waits for III.7.

For two hundred years, "joining with oxygen" had a name: oxidation. Iron is oxidised to rust. Wax is oxidised in a candle. An apple's flesh is oxidised brown.

**Oxidation, first pass: a thing joining with oxygen. Rust, browning and burning are all oxidations.**

Keep the words "first pass". Tomorrow the definition gets bigger, and oxygen turns out to be a guest star, not the point.
