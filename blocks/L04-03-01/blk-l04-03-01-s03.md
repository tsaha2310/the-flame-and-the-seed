The spirit's oxygen sits between the end carbon and a hydrogen: an O-H hung on the chain. That bond is polar, from II.3, and it can make hydrogen bonds with water. A short chain with one O-H mixes with water. The group is called a hydroxyl. A molecule carrying one is an alcohol.

Vinegar's end carbon holds two oxygens: one by a double bond, and one as an O-H. That arrangement lets the hydrogen come off as an ion, from III.7. It is sour. The group is called a carboxyl. A molecule carrying one is an organic acid.

A small cluster of atoms that gives a molecule a particular behaviour, wherever it sits, is a functional group. The hydroxyl always makes a spot that loves water. The carboxyl always makes a spot that gives a hydrogen ion. The rest of the molecule, the hydrocarbon skeleton, decides how oily the whole thing is.

The long-chain alcohol and acid still have their groups, still polar at one end, still faintly sour. But their long oily tails now outweigh the one polar spot, and they no longer mix with water.

**A functional group is a small cluster that does the same job wherever it is hung. A hydroxyl loves water. A carboxyl gives a hydrogen ion.**
