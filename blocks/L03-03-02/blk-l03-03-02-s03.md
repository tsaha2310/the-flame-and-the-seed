In oxygen, each sodium atom gives up its outer electron and the oxygen takes it. Sodium ions and oxide ions form a white lattice.

In chlorine, each sodium atom gives up its outer electron and the chlorine takes it. Sodium ions and chloride ions form a white lattice. Salt.

The same thing happened to the sodium both times. It lost an electron. Oxygen and chlorine did the same job: they took it. Oxygen was never the point. Oxygen is just very good at taking electrons, which is why it is in so many of these reactions.

So the definition grows. Oxidation is losing electrons. The steel wool was oxidised because iron lost electrons to oxygen. The sodium was oxidised in both jars.

And every loss is someone's gain. The atom that takes the electrons is reduced. Chlorine was reduced to chloride. Oxygen was reduced to oxide.

The two always happen together, because an electron that leaves one atom must arrive at another. A reaction where electrons move from one thing to another is a redox reaction: reduction and oxidation, in one word.

**Oxidation is losing electrons. Reduction is gaining them. Oxygen is just very good at taking them, which is why it got the name.**

Every combustion, every rusting, every displacement from III.1, the nail in copper sulphate, is a redox. Iron gave electrons; copper ions took them.
