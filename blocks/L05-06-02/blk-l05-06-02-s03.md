The bucket is NAD+. It is built from two nucleotides joined by their phosphates, from IV.6, with a ring on one end that can hold two extra electrons and a hydrogen. Empty, it is NAD+, with a positive charge. Full, carrying two electrons, it is NADH.

Each time the cell strips a pair of electrons off a carbon, the carbon's score goes up, from III.3: it has been oxidised. The electrons do not go to oxygen. They go into an NAD+, which becomes NADH and floats away with them. NADH is a molecule that carries electrons from one place to another: an electron carrier.

The carrier holds the electrons partway down the Hill. It took them from carbon, a poor puller, and it will hand them on only to a better puller than itself. Water is not; the electrons stay in the bucket. Oxygen is, by a lot, but the cell does not let NADH meet oxygen directly, because that would be a drop of ten coins in one go. NADH unloads at the top of a staircase, and that staircase is the next module.

**NAD+ is an empty bucket for two electrons. Stripping a carbon fills it into NADH. NADH carries the electrons partway down the Hill, to where the staircase begins.**

The scoreboard from Book III is now a story with three characters: the carbon that loses, the carrier that holds, and the staircase that finally hands them to oxygen.
