Two H2 and one O2 on the left. Two H2O on the right. Four hydrogens each side, two oxygens each side. The pans level.

Written out: 2 H2 + O2 gives 2 H2O. This is a chemical equation. The numbers in front are how many of each molecule. The little numbers inside are how many atoms in each molecule, and they can never be changed, because H2O is what water is. You balance with the numbers in front only.

An equation whose atom counts match on both sides is balanced. An unbalanced one claims that atoms appeared or vanished, and Book I says they cannot.

Methane burning: CH4 + 2 O2 gives CO2 + 2 H2O. One carbon, four hydrogens, four oxygens on each side.

**A chemical equation is an atom count. It is balanced when every element has the same count on both sides, because atoms are never made or lost.**

The equation does one more thing you will use constantly. It gives the recipe. Two hydrogens for every oxygen. One methane for every two oxygens. That is why the fixed recipes of Book I exist.
