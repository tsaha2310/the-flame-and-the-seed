The start sign is the promoter: a short DNA sequence, just before a gene, shaped so that RNA polymerase's pocket grips it. Without a promoter, the copyist has nowhere to land, and the gene is never copied. The promoter is also where a gene is switched on and off, which is VI.6.

The copyist moves in one direction, adding RNA letters at the growing copy's three-prime end, so it must read its template from three-prime to five-prime. Of the two DNA strands, the one it reads is the template strand. The other, which the copy ends up matching letter for letter with U for T, is not read at all for this gene. A different gene, further along, may use the other strand as its template; the two strands take turns depending on which way each gene faces.

Each gene, then, has a promoter, a start, a body and an end signal. The copyist grips the promoter, unzips, reads the template, writes the copy, reaches the end signal, and lets go.

**A promoter is a start sign the copyist grips. It reads the template strand, three-prime to five-prime, and writes an mRNA that matches the other strand with U for T.**
