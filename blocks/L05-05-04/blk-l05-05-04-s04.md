The walking protein is myosin. The track is actin, a chain of beads that a muscle is full of. One cycle, one coin, one step.

- ATP binds the head. The fit bends the head, and the bend makes it let go of the track.
- The head splits the ATP into ADP and phosphate, and keeps both in its pocket. Splitting does not pull. It cocks: the head swings back into a strained shape, like a drawn bow.
- The cocked head touches the track further along and binds.
- The phosphate leaves the pocket. That release lets the strained head snap forward, dragging the track with it. That is the pull: a few nanometres, a few piconewtons.
- ADP leaves. The head is stuck fast to the track in its relaxed shape, until the next ATP arrives to release it.

The coin was spent on a shape change. The shape change was coupled to a pull on the track. Chemistry became work, from I.2, through Lock and Shape.

Notice what pulls: not the splitting of ATP, but the release of its pieces, letting a strained protein relax. The strain was stored in the fold, exactly as a bond's energy is stored in an arrangement.

**Myosin binds ATP and lets go, splits it and cocks, grabs the track, releases the phosphate and pulls, releases ADP and holds. One coin, one step. A coin moves a leg by bending a fold.**

The same walking trick, with different proteins, carries vesicles along tracks from V.3, pulls the copied tapes apart in Book VIII, and beats the tail of a sperm.
