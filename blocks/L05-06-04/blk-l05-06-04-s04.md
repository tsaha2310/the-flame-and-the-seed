NAD+ is not used up by glycolysis; it is filled. A filled bucket cannot take another pair of electrons. So the pipeline needs its buckets emptied as fast as it fills them, or it stops at the filling step.

In a cell with oxygen, the power plant empties them: NADH hands its electrons to the staircase, which hands them down to oxygen, and NAD+ comes back empty. That is the next module, and it is where most of the thirty coins are made.

In a cell without oxygen, there is no staircase to empty into. The cell must dump the electrons somewhere else, onto pyruvate itself, just to get its buckets back. That is the module after next, and it is what dahi, idli batter and a sprinting calf muscle have in common.

**Glycolysis fills buckets and cannot empty them. Something else must, or the pipeline jams at the filling step. With air, the power plant empties them. Without, the cell improvises.**
