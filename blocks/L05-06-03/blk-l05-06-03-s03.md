The ten-step pipeline is glycolysis: "sugar splitting". It runs in the cell's open room, which is called the cytosol, the water outside all the organelles. Every cell that has ever lived runs it, bacteria included. It is the oldest part of the staircase.

Steps one to three: invest. Two coins are spent tagging the glucose at both ends. A doubly tagged sugar is clamped, from V.4, and strained enough to break.

Step four: split. The six-carbon sugar breaks into two three-carbon pieces.

Steps six to ten, on each piece: earn. A carbon is stripped and an NAD+ is filled into NADH, from V.6 L02. Then the piece's tags are handed to ADP, twice, making two ATP per piece, four in all. What is left is a three-carbon molecule with its carbons scored a little higher than they started: pyruvate.

Net for one glucose: two ATP, two NADH, two pyruvate. No oxygen touched. The pyruvate's carbons still hold most of glucose's electrons; the pipeline has taken only the first small steps down.

**Glycolysis splits one glucose into two pyruvate in ten steps in the cytosol. Spend two coins, earn four, fill two buckets. No oxygen needed. Invest before you earn.**

The pipeline keeps flowing because each product is taken away by the next enzyme, from Le Chatelier in III.6. Stop taking pyruvate away, and glycolysis backs up and stops.
