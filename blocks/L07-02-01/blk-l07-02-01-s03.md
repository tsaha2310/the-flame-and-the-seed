A nerve cell is a neuron: a cell body with branches that receive, and one long cable that carries, from a fingertip to the spine, or from the spine to a muscle.

At rest, its wall is a charged battery, from V.2 L03. The pump keeps sodium out and potassium in. Potassium leak channels let a little potassium drift out down its gradient, carrying positive charge out and leaving the inside negative. That negative pulls potassium back, and at about minus seventy millivolts the two balance. Sodium leaks in only slightly, because its channels are few and mostly shut. The steady voltage across a resting neuron's wall is its resting voltage. It is the gradient from Book V, read as volts.

That voltage is stored. Nothing is moving along the cable yet. The cell is a wire with the switch off, and the stored charge is what the message will spend.

**A neuron at rest holds about minus seventy millivolts across its wall, inside negative, because pumps pile potassium inside and leak channels let a little out. That stored voltage is the message's fuel.**
