The first night of the monsoon. The rain has been hammering the tin roof of Ajji's verandah for an hour.

Then the power goes. The fan slows. The fridge stops humming. It is completely dark.

Ajji strikes a match and lights the brass diya on the shelf. One small flame. It leans when the wind comes through the door, then stands up again.

Kabir, your eight-year-old cousin, has been staring at it for a full minute. That is a long time for Kabir.

"Is the flame alive?"

You laugh. Of course it is not.

"Why not?" he says. "It eats the oil. Look, the oil is going down. It breathes. If I put a glass over it, it dies. It grows when Ajji adds oil. It moves. And watch."

He holds a matchstick to the flame. The matchstick catches. "It made a baby."

You open your mouth to answer. Nothing comes out.
