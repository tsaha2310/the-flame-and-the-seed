Every bond has a price: the energy needed to pull the two atoms apart, per mole. That price is its bond energy. Breaking any bond costs energy. Every bond, every time. There is no such thing as a bond that gives out energy when it breaks.

Making a bond pays the same price back. Two atoms falling into a grip release exactly what it would cost to separate them.

Methane burning: pay about 2640 to break four C-H and two O=O. Earn about 3450 from making two C=O and four O-H. Net: about 810 earned. The products sit lower on the Hill than the reactants. The difference comes out as heat and light. That is the blue flame.

A reaction that gives out heat is exothermic. Its enthalpy goes down. Burning, rusting, the fizz, the seed's slow burn: all exothermic.

A reaction that takes in heat is endothermic. Its enthalpy goes up. Water splitting: pay to break four O-H, earn from two H-H and one O=O, and the earning is less. The battery makes up the difference. Melting ice is endothermic too.

**Breaking bonds costs. Making bonds pays. A reaction gives out heat when the bonds it makes are worth more than the bonds it breaks.**

The bonds in the products of burning, C=O and O-H, are among the strongest there are. That is why so many things burn, and why fuels are worth having.
