The ribosome's one check is codon against anticodon. It never checks the bead. So the charging enzymes carry the whole responsibility for the code, and real ones proofread: they eject a wrong bead before attaching it, most of the time.

Deleting one letter shifts the frame and ruins the chain. Deleting three removes one bead and keeps the frame, so a protein can survive a three-letter loss and almost never a one-letter one.

Bacteria and humans both have ribosomes of two subunits, three slots, mostly RNA. But the bacterial one is smaller and its folds differ in detail. Several antibiotics, from soil fungi and bacteria that use them against rivals, fit a bacterial slot and jam it. They do not fit ours. That is why a course of tablets stops an infection and leaves you standing. It is also why bacteria that change the slot's shape by one letter survive the tablets: Book VIII.

**The ribosome trusts the hook; charging enzymes carry the code. One letter deleted shifts the frame; three do not. A bacterial ribosome differs from ours enough for a molecule to jam it and not us.**
