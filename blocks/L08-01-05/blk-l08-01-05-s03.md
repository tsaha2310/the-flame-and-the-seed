Kabir watches his zero-error population, perfect and identical, die at the first change.

"They were all the best. And then the best was wrong."

"And nothing else was there to be right."

**Predict first:** what does a zero-error population do when its world changes?

<!-- STEP id="run" -->
**Turn the dial.** At zero, every copy is identical. The population is perfectly suited to its world, and when the world shifts, every individual is equally unsuited. There is no variation to draw on. It shrinks and goes extinct at the first shift. At one in a thousand, the genome collects several errors per copy; most are harmless, but a few break something every generation, and the broken ones accumulate; fitness slides downhill and the population collapses in a few hundred generations, with no shift needed. In between, at a rate that gives most copies no errors and a few copies one, the population stays healthy, carries a little variation, and when the world shifts, some individuals happen to suit it, and their descendants take over. It adapts. Too cold, too hot, just right.

<!-- STEP id="nails" -->
**What this nails:** Imperfect copying is a requirement for evolution, not a flaw in it. A copier that never errs makes a population that cannot change. A sloppy one makes a population that falls apart. Life sits in the narrow band between.
