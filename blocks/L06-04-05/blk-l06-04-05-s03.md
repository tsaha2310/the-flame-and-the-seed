**Turn the dial.** The wrong-charged tRNA sails through. Its anticodon pairs with AAA, the ribosome checks the pairing and is satisfied, and alanine goes into the chain where lysine belonged. The ribosome cannot tell; it never sees the bead. One letter deleted: every bead after it is wrong and the chain ends early. Three deleted: one bead missing, the rest correct. The jamming molecule wedges into a slot in the bacterial ribosome and stops it. The same molecule does not fit the human ribosome's slot, which differs by a few atoms, and the human cell carries on. The bacterium, unable to make protein, stops growing and dies.

<!-- STEP id="nails" -->
**What this nails:** The ribosome trusts the charging enzymes completely, which is why they must be accurate. Frame is everything. And a ribosome's exact shape, different in bacteria and in us, is why an antibiotic can kill an infection without killing the patient.
