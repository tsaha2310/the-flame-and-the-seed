"In 1971," says Ajji, "millions of people fled across the border into West Bengal. In the camps, cholera broke out. Cholera kills by diarrhoea: the gut pours out water and salt faster than anyone can drink. The treatment was a drip into a vein, and there were not enough drips, or doctors, or clean needles, for thousands of people a day."

"A young doctor named Dilip Mahalanabis had a different idea. Salt, sugar and water, by mouth, in exact amounts. The death rate fell from thirty in a hundred to three."

"Why the sugar?" says Kabir. "They were losing salt."

"That is this whole module. First, what can cross the wall at all."
