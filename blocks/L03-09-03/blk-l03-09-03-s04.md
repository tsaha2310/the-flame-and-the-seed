The energy a battery delivers is the volts, the steepness, multiplied by the charge that fell, the number of electrons. A steep fall for a few electrons is little. A gentle fall for a great many is more.

And that energy is free energy. The volts of a cell are the free-energy drop of its redox reaction, per unit of charge. A wire and a meter can measure the same number the Hill was drawing.

The two beakers keep the two half-reactions clean. Zinc dissolves in its own beaker. Copper ions plate out as metal in theirs. The strips change mass exactly as the charge predicts: one mole of electrons dissolves half a mole of zinc.

The paper strip is the salt bridge. As zinc ions pile up in the left beaker and copper ions are used up in the right, the beakers would build up charge and the flow would stop. The bridge lets ions cross to balance it. Pull it out and the LED dies at once.

This two-beaker design is the Daniell cell. It ran the world's first telegraphs.

**Energy from a battery is volts times charge. The volts are the free-energy drop of its reaction, and the charge is how many electrons fell.**
