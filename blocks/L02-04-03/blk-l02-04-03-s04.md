Every time a particle hits the wall, it pushes. Billions of hits a second add up to a steady push. That push is gas pressure. Hotter particles hit harder and more often. More particles hit more often. A smaller box means more hits per bit of wall. All three raise the pressure. That is why a sealed bottle of water fizzes harder in the sun, and why a tyre goes hard on a hot road.

With the top open, some particles leave. Not any particles. Only the ones jiggling hard enough to break free of their neighbours' pull. The fastest ones.

Take the fastest runners off a team, and the average speed of the team drops. Take the fastest molecules out of a puddle, and its temperature drops. That is why evaporation cools. Evaporation is escape from the surface, one fast molecule at a time.

The matka sweats through its clay. The sweat evaporates. The water loses its fastest molecules through the wall all night. By morning it is cool.

**Gas pressure is the drumming of particles on a wall. Evaporation cools, because only the fastest particles escape and the slow ones stay behind.**

Sweat does the same on your skin. So does a wet cloth on a fevered forehead.
