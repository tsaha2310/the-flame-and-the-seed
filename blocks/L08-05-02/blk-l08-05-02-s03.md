Rock layers: the remains or traces of living things preserved in rock are fossils. Lower layers are older, from the way sediment settles. The lowest layers with life hold only single cells. Fish appear before land animals; land animals before mammals; no rabbit has ever been found in a layer older than the first fish. The order is the same on every continent.

Forelimbs: a human arm, a bat's wing, a whale's flipper and a horse's leg do different jobs and have the same bones in the same order: one upper bone, two lower, a cluster of wrist bones, five sets of fingers, stretched or shrunk or fused. A structure shared by different species because they inherited it from a common ancestor is a homologous structure. A bat's wing is a hand.

The code: every species uses the same codon table, from VI.3, with tiny exceptions. Any table would do; there is no chemical reason AUG must be methionine. One shared arbitrary choice means one origin.

DNA: a human gene matches a chimpanzee's in about 99 letters in 100; a mouse's, about 85; a moong's, perhaps 60 for a shared gene; a bacterium's, less. The nearer two species were placed in the old groupings by looks, the more letters they share. That is what descent with copying errors predicts, from VIII.1: the longer since two lines parted, the more slips have piled up between them.

**Fossils appear in a fixed order; different limbs share the same bones; every species reads the same code; and DNA similarity falls with distance on the tree. Four independent witnesses to shared ancestry.**
