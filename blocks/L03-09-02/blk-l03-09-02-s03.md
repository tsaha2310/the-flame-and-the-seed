Every pair's reading is a difference of two heights. Magnesium sits high. Zinc lower. Iron lower still. Copper near the bottom. Silver below copper.

Voltage, which in Book I was "how hard the battery shoves", is now something better: the steepness of the electron's fall. The higher the giver on the ladder and the lower the taker, the steeper the drop, the more volts.

The ladder of metals by how easily they give electrons is the reactivity series. It is the same ladder you found in III.1 with the nail, now with numbers on the rungs. A metal higher up will push a metal lower down out of its compound, will burn more fiercely, will rust more readily, and will make more volts against any partner below it.

**Voltage is the steepness of an electron's fall. The reactivity series is the ladder it falls down, and a battery's volts are the gap between two rungs.**

The AA cell in Kabir's torch is 1.5 volts: zinc against a manganese compound. A lemon cell with zinc and copper is about 1 volt, and can barely light an LED. A car battery is six lead cells in a row, 12 volts.
