Salt in water: water's negative oxygen corners crowd round each sodium ion, and its positive hydrogen ends crowd round each chloride. Ion by ion, the lattice is pulled apart, and each ion floats off wrapped in a shell of water. The ions are still there. They are spread out and surrounded.

Sugar in water: sugar has many places where an oxygen holds a hydrogen, like water does. Water molecules make hydrogen bonds to them, and each sugar molecule is lifted off the crystal and carried away, whole.

Wax in water: nothing. Wax is long chains of carbon and hydrogen, with no partial charges. Water's molecules hold each other so hard that the wax is squeezed out. It floats.

Wax in oil: dissolves. Oil and wax are the same kind of molecule, and dispersion forces between them are as good as within them.

Salt in oil: nothing. Oil has no charged corners to pull ions off the lattice.

The thing that dissolves is the solute. The liquid it dissolves in is the solvent. The mixed result is a solution. Water is called the universal solvent because its charged corners can grip so many things, but it is not universal. It cannot grip oil.

**Like dissolves like. Water pulls apart anything with charges or hydrogen bonds. Oil dissolves only things as uncharged as itself.**
