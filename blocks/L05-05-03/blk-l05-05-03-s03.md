The end-clipper is glycogen phosphorylase. It takes a bead off a chain end, and instead of splitting the link with water, from IV.4, it splits it with a phosphate. The bead comes off already tagged, the way hexokinase would have tagged it, for free. No coin spent. Withdrawal is cheaper than deposit.

Phosphorylase works only at ends, and only down to four beads from a fork. Then it stalls. That is when the debranching enzyme steps in: it moves the last three beads of a stub onto a neighbouring chain, then cuts the fourth off by ordinary hydrolysis. The fork is gone and the chain is straight again, with ends ready for phosphorylase.

Hundreds of ends means hundreds of phosphorylases working at once, and the bank pays out fast enough for a sprint. This is why the brancher earned its coins yesterday.

Phosphorylase is allosteric, from V.4 L05. It is throttled by the cell's coin level: plenty of ATP, and it slows; spent coins piling up, and it runs. And it has a switch that Book VII turns from outside, when a message from the blood arrives.

**Glycogen phosphorylase clips beads off the ends, pre-tagged, coin-free. The debranching enzyme clears the forks. Many ends, fast withdrawal.**
