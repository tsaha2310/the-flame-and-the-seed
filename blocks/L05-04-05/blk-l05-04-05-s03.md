The first enzyme has two pockets. One is its active site. The other is a notch on the far side, shaped for the pipeline's final product. When product is plentiful, product molecules bump into the notch and sit there. The fit bends the enzyme, from Lock and Shape, and the bend reaches across the fold and squeezes the active site slightly shut. The first enzyme slows. Less enters the pipeline. When product is scarce, the notch is empty, the enzyme relaxes, and the pipeline runs full.

An enzyme whose shape, and so whose speed, is changed by a molecule binding somewhere other than its active site is allosteric: "other shape". The notch is an allosteric site.

When the thing that slows an enzyme is the product of its own pipeline, that is feedback inhibition. The pipeline regulates itself. No message, no manager. The product's own concentration, from the Crowd, sets how often the notch is filled, and the notch sets the rate.

**An allosteric enzyme has a second pocket. A molecule sitting there bends the fold and changes the active site. When that molecule is the pipeline's own product, the pipeline throttles itself: feedback inhibition.**

This is the thermostat idea. Book VII builds bodies out of it.
