The inverter is beeping its low-battery warning and the house is dark again. Kabir has a torch battery, two lengths of wire, and the little LED from a broken toy.

He touches the wires together: the LED lights. He puts the two bare ends into a glass of water, a finger apart: nothing.

"Water is a conductor," he says. "Everyone knows that. You must not touch a switch with wet hands."

Ajji says nothing, and puts six glasses on the table.
