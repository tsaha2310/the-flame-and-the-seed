Kabir's one-tower leaf fills a dam and makes no NADPH and no oxygen. He builds the second tower and both appear.

**Predict first:** how high does the electron get, and can it reach NADPH?

<!-- STEP id="run" -->
**Turn the dial.** One photon lifts the electron to the top of the first tower. From there it can fall and pump ions, but it lands well below NADPH's rung and cannot fill the carrier. The dam fills; no NADPH. Some early bacteria still live this way, from a sulphur spring, and they cannot split water either, because a single tower's hole is not a strong enough puller.

<!-- STEP id="nails" -->
**What this nails:** Two lifts are what get an electron from water, the tightest-held electrons in the kitchen, all the way up to a carrier that can make sugar. One lift is a solar heater; two is a solar factory.
