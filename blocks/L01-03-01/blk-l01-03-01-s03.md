Every salt gave its own colour. Kitchen salt, bright yellow. The potassium salt, faint lilac. Chuna, a brick orange-red. Copper sulphate, green-blue. Boric powder, apple green.

The colour does not depend on the shape of the grains, or how much you sprinkle, or how hot the flame is. A tiny pinch of kitchen salt gives the same yellow as a spoonful. It is a fingerprint.

And the fingerprint belongs to the element, not the compound. Sodium in kitchen salt, sodium in the eno sachet, sodium in washing soda: all yellow. The chemist who first used this trick, Robert Bunsen, found two new elements in 1860 just by seeing colours in a flame that no known element gave.

**Each element has its own flame colour, fixed and unchanging. Something inside the atom chooses the colour, and cannot choose differently.**

Why yellow for sodium? Why not red, or any colour at all? Hold that question. It takes three more lessons to answer, and the answer is worth it.
