Same bead. Two ways to point the bridge.

Alpha links make each ring tilt a little relative to the last, and a chain of them coils into a helix, a loose spiral. This chain is starch: the roti, the potato, the rice, the moong seed's store. It is coiled, so it is easy to get at, and the cutter in your saliva fits the alpha link.

Beta links make each ring flip relative to the last, and the chain lies flat and straight. Flat chains stack on top of each other and hydrogen-bond along their whole length into fibres. This chain is cellulose: the shirt, the table, the walls of every plant. It is the most common molecule on Earth that a living thing makes. Your cutter does not fit the beta link, so it passes through you as fibre. A cow keeps microbes whose cutters do.

Alpha links with branches every ten beads or so make a bushy tree of a molecule: glycogen. Animals store glucose this way, in liver and muscle, and the many branch ends mean many places to add a bead or take one off quickly. Book V opens a bank account with it.

**One bead, three chains. Alpha coils into starch and glycogen, which are food. Beta lies flat into cellulose, which is a wall.**
