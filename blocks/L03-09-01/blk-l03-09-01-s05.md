Kabir's dry battery does nothing. He drips salt water in and it lights. He adds a note to the notebook: "batteries are wet inside".

**Predict first:** does the LED light?

<!-- STEP id="run" -->
**Turn the dial.** No. Electrons cannot pile up on the copper strip with nothing to take them, and zinc cannot dissolve into nothing. The circuit is only half a circuit. Add the salt water back and the LED lights: ions in the water complete the loop.

<!-- STEP id="nails" -->
**What this nails:** A battery needs both a wire, for electrons, and a liquid, for ions. That is why the salt water lit the LED in Book I and why a dry battery has a damp paste inside.
