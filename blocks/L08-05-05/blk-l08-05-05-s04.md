Lamarck was right that species change and wrong about how. What an animal does in its life changes its body and not its gametes. The gametes are made by meiosis from cells set aside early, and they carry the DNA, which nothing the body does can edit toward a goal. A blacksmith's arms do not give his children strong arms; his children inherit his spellings and grow arms of their own.

The idea that a species can produce the mutation it needs is Lamarck's idea in new clothes, and it fails the same way. Copying slips are random in direction, from VIII.1. The finch that got the big-beak slip got it by chance; the ones that did not, starved. Evolution has no foresight. It has variation, and a sieve.

And a population with no variation cannot evolve at all. Selection removes; it never adds. If nothing varies, nothing is left to choose from when the world changes.

**Use and disuse are not inherited: the body cannot write to the tape. Mutations are random, and no species can order the one it needs. Selection is not random, and it needs variation to work on. Nothing evolves because it needs to.**

One honest footnote. Some marks on the tape's spools, from VI.5's chromatin, can pass through a few generations and affect which genes are open. That is real, small, and reversible, and it does not change the spellings. It is a wrinkle, not Lamarck's return.
