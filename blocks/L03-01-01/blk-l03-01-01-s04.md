Sealed, the balance does not move. The gas has mass, and it is still in the bottle. Open, the balance drops as the gas leaves, and that is the whole of what Kabir saw: mass leaving, not mass vanishing. Lavoisier's rule holds.

In the cut-away, the soda's atoms and the nimbu's atoms let go of their partners and take new ones. A hydrogen from the acid, a carbon and two oxygens from the soda, together become water and carbon dioxide gas. Nothing was hiding. The gas was made from pieces that were already there, held a different way.

That is a chemical reaction: atoms letting go of some grips and taking others. The things you start with are the reactants. The things you end with are the products.

Sugar in water is not one. The cut-away shows sugar molecules floating apart from each other, whole, every bond still in place. Same molecules, spread out.

The nail in copper sulphate is one. Iron atoms leave the nail and enter the liquid as ions; copper ions leave the liquid and settle on the nail as metal. The nail turns pink and the blue fades.

**A chemical reaction is a re-gripping. The atoms are the same before and after. Only who holds whom has changed.**

The signs you can see: a gas appears, a colour changes, heat comes out or is taken in, a solid forms in a clear liquid, a smell appears. Any of these means grips have changed.
