Breaking ATP to ADP and phosphate is exergonic, from III.8: free energy falls. Tagging glucose with a phosphate is endergonic: free energy rises, by less. Side by side, ATP breaks slowly into water and the fall is wasted as heat; the glucose is untouched. Two reactions in one glass are not coupled.

Hexokinase couples them. Its pocket binds glucose first; the fit bends the fold, from Lock and Shape, and only then can ATP bind alongside, with its end phosphate held right against the glucose's hydroxyl. The phosphate moves across. ATP's fall has paid for glucose's lift, in one pocket, with no free phosphate ever loose in the water. The pair together is downhill: fall bigger than lift, difference wasted as heat, exactly the waterwheel's rules.

The wheel is the enzyme. It holds the two reactions in one place so that the fall cannot happen without the lift. With ATP alone in the pocket, hexokinase does almost nothing; the fold does not close properly without glucose. That is how it avoids wasting coins on water.

**Coupling through ATP: one enzyme holds the falling ATP and the rising substrate in one pocket, so the fall pays for the lift. Exergonic plus endergonic, together, downhill.**

Nearly every anabolic step in the seedling, from V.4 L01, is paid this way. A different enzyme for each, the same coin for all.
