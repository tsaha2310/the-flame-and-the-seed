A swallowed vesicle, food or bacterium inside, is carried to the acid bag from V.1 L03 and fuses with it. Inside, at pH 5, cutters that only work in acid, from III.7, take the contents apart into sugars, amino acids and fatty acids, which pass out through the bag's wall into the cell. The acid bag is a lysosome: "splitting body". A bacterium swallowed by the macrophage ends here, in pieces.

Meanwhile the cell has made things that are for the outside: a hormone, a digestive cutter, a bit of new fence. These are packed into vesicles on the workshop floor. A vesicle is carried to the outer wall and fuses with it, from the inside. The vesicle's contents spill outside, and the vesicle's wall becomes part of the outer wall. That is exocytosis: "out of the cell". It is endocytosis run backwards, and every bit of wall that went in as a vesicle comes back out as one.

So the wall is recycled. In, to the lysosome; out, from the workshop floor. The surface stays the same size because the two rates match.

**A swallowed vesicle fuses with the lysosome, where acid cutters take its contents apart. Outgoing vesicles fuse with the outer wall and spill their load: exocytosis. Wall in equals wall out.**
