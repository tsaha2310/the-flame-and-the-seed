Three ways for atoms to hold together are now on the table, and you have seen two.

Sodium gives, chlorine takes: a steal. That makes ions, and ions make salt water conduct.

Copper's atoms share their loose electrons with the whole lump: a pool. That makes a metal.

And sugar? Sugar does not conduct, so no ions. Sugar is not a metal. So there must be a third grip.

"Share," says Ajji. "Between two atoms, and nobody else. That is the one the seed is made of."
